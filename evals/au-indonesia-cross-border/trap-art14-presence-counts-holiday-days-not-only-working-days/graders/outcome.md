  PASS only if the answer says all of:
  1. Art 14(1)(b) looks at the individual's presence in Indonesia for periods exceeding 120 days in any 12-month period, not only working days. On the usual (OECD Commentary) reading of physical presence, holiday days count, so presence is 130 days and is over the limit. It should acknowledge that the treaty does not define a day and that this is an interpretive aid.
  2. If the limit is exceeded, Indonesia may tax only so much of the income as is derived from the activities carried out in Indonesia (roughly the 100 working days' worth), not the whole income.
  3. Australia still taxes its resident's worldwide income, with relief by foreign income tax offset for Indonesian tax correctly imposed. It also flags the separate fixed-base rule (Art 14(1)(a)), which needs no day count, and suggests a registered tax agent review.
  FAIL if it agrees that only the 100 working days count, says the whole income becomes taxable in Indonesia, or applies a 183-day threshold.
