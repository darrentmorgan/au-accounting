  PASS only if the answer says all of:
  1. The Australia-Indonesia agreement's individual tie-breaker (Art 4(3)) runs in this order: permanent home available; then habitual abode; then closer economic and personal relations. It is not the OECD Model order (which puts personal and economic relations before habitual abode).
  2. There is no nationality step and no mutual agreement step for individuals in this agreement, so citizenship does not decide the question.
  3. It does not confidently name one country as the sole treaty residence on these facts: a home is available in both, so the habitual abode step (or the closer-relations step after it) turns on facts, and it points to a registered tax agent or the competent authority process for a determination. It may say the facts (family, company and banking in Adelaide) point towards Australia at the last step as an indication only.
  It may add that the tie-breaker only applies if the person is a resident of both countries under each country's own domestic law, and that Indonesian domestic residence is a question for an Indonesian adviser.
  FAIL if it says the OECD order applies, that nationality is a step, or that citizenship decides.
