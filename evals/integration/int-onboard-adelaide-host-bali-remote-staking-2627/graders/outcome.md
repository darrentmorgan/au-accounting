criteria: |
  PASS only if the answer is a checklist working paper covering ALL of the following, correctly:
  1. Airbnb unit (short-stay): declare the GROSS booking income before platform fees (the ATO receives platform reports under the Sharing Economy Reporting Regime, so net payouts would not match) and claim platform fees, agent fees, advertising and cleaning after guest stays as direct costs; ownership costs (interest, rates, insurance, repairs, depreciation, capital works) are apportioned for private use or nights not available for rent on commercial terms; if the owner, family or friends use it for holidays free or cheaply it is a holiday home and the mainly-for-rent test (ITAA 1997 s 26-50) can deny ownership costs outright; the owner's own travel to clean or inspect is not deductible for an individual not in business (s 26-31); second-hand furniture depreciation is denied for a residential rental (s 40-27). Records: booking calendar, nights blocked, pricing and enquiry evidence, platform statements.
  2. GST for the unit: short-stay rent from a whole unit or house is normally input taxed residential rent (no GST, no credits, not counted in GST turnover), but a hotel-like operation could be commercial residential premises, which is a question for a registered tax or BAS agent, not something to decide here. It must NOT say the user must register for GST simply because income comes through a platform.
  3. Crypto: staking rewards are ordinary income at their AUD market value when received (and that value is the cost base of the reward tokens); a swap from one coin to another is a CGT disposal at the AUD value received (not tax free until cashing out); 12-month discount where held 12 months or more (a swap restarts the clock on the coin received); moving between own wallets is not a disposal; keep AUD-valued records of every transaction, not just exchange statements (ATO data matching). It should say drafts (airdrops TR 2026/D1, wrapping TD 2026/D2) are drafts if it mentions them, and escalate DeFi, mining or trading-business facts if they arise.
  4. Bali stay and residency: it must NOT conclude residency either way from 5 months; the user is likely still an Australian resident on these facts but this is for a registered agent to confirm (facts: home, family, employment, intention); a resident is taxed on worldwide income. On the treaty: Art 15 (dependent services) lets Indonesia tax the pay for work done in Indonesia unless ALL four conditions are met, the first being presence not exceeding 120 days in any 12-month period, so roughly 150 days in Bali fails that condition (the test is a rolling 12-month period, not the income year and not 183 days); any Indonesian tax correctly imposed would be relieved through the foreign income tax offset (ITAA 1997 Div 770), which needs evidence of the tax paid and the rupiah converted to AUD with the ATO published rates; Indonesian domestic tax and Indonesian residency are outside what it can verify and go to an Indonesian adviser.
  5. What not to try alone (escalations): residency determination, Indonesian domestic tax, any business-scale letting, entity ownership, and DeFi or trading-business crypto; and the answer states that it is a working paper for a registered tax agent to review, with sections for figures used, assumptions, risk flags and refusals/escalations, ending with the review line.
  FAIL if it says the Bali stay makes the user a non-resident, uses a 183-day rule to conclude Indonesia cannot tax the salary, says swaps are not taxable, says net platform payouts are what to declare, says GST must be charged on the rent or that registration is required because of platform income, or is not laid out as a working paper.
