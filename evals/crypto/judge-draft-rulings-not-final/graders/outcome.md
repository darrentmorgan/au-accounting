It says TR 2026/D1 (airdrops) and TD 2026/D2 (wrapping) are drafts (issued August 2026, open for comment), not final rulings and not law, and that the ATO web pages reflecting the same positions do not change that status. It may note that reasonable good-faith reliance on a draft can protect against interest and penalties but not the tax itself.
  2. For the airdrop it gives the draft's preliminary view for a non-business investor who did nothing to earn it: not ordinary income on receipt; the airdropped token is its own CGT asset with a cost base of the market value at receipt, about $2,000 (5,000 x $0.40); later sale is a CGT event.
  3. It labels this treatment as preliminary and suggests confirming with a registered tax agent or watching for the final ruling.
  FAIL if it calls the drafts final or binding, or says the airdrop is ordinary income to this investor, or gives a nil cost base for an established token that had a market price.
