## Part A: GST points

### A1. GST-free export of services, s38-190(1) item 2
- Rule (KNOWN): item 2 tests the recipient, not where the service is consumed. The supply must be made to a **non-resident who is not in Australia when the thing supplied is done**, AND either (a) it is neither a supply of work physically performed on goods situated in Australia when the work is done nor a supply directly connected with real property in Australia, OR (b) the non-resident acquires the thing in carrying on its enterprise but is not registered or required to be registered.
- "Consumed/effectively used or enjoyed outside Australia" is a different test: item 3 (recipient not in Australia when the thing is done AND effective use or enjoyment outside Australia). A guide that describes item 2 as "consumed outside Australia" is conflating items 2 and 3.
- Extra limits: s38-190(2A) (not GST-free if the acquisition relates to input-taxed Australian real property supplies), s38-190(3) (item 2 supply under an agreement with a non-resident but provided to another entity in Australia is not GST-free), s38-190(4) (deemed "not in Australia" for agreements with an Australian resident where the supply is provided to an entity outside Australia; applies to items 2 and 3 context per the 2010 amendments). Who is "not in Australia" is in GSTR 2004/7.
- Sources: GSTR 2000/31 (consolidated, reproduces items 1 to 5 at para 23, ss38-190(2A), (3)): https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/pdf?DocID=GST%2FGSTR200031%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001&PiT=20150722000001&filename=law%2Fview%2Fpdf%2Fpbr%2Fgstr2000-031c4.pdf ; GSTR 2002/D1 excerpt of item 2 (draft, same text). GST Act structure: https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00446/latest/text/1 (TOC only; operative text of 38-190 taken from the ATO ruling reproduction, not from the Act page itself).

### A2. Supply of a going concern, s38-325
- Rule (KNOWN): s38-325(1) has **3 conditions**: (a) supply is for consideration; (b) recipient is registered or required to be registered; (c) supplier and recipient have agreed in writing that the supply is of a going concern. Separately, s38-325(2) defines "supply of a going concern" with **2 elements**: (a) supplier supplies all things necessary for continued operation of an enterprise; (b) supplier carries on, or will carry on, the enterprise until the day of supply. Total: 5 requirements in practice (ATO's GST and taxis page lists five). A guide saying "four conditions" or "three" without the split is imprecise; safest wording is "3 conditions in s38-325(1) plus the 2-limb definition in s38-325(2)".
- Sources: GSTR 2002/5 paras 9, 12 (ATO legal database, PiT 2024): https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/pdf?DocID=GST%2FGSTR20025%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001&PiT=20240214000001&filename=law%2Fview%2Fpdf%2Fpbr%2Fgstr2002-005c6.pdf ; ATO ID 2012/54 https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?DocID=AID%2FAID201254%2F00001 ; ATO GST and taxis (five-item list) https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/in-detail/your-industry/motor-vehicle-and-transport/gst-and-taxis

### A3. Bad debts, Division 21
- Rule (KNOWN): the "overdue for 12 months or more" limb is available to **both** parties, on the accrual (non-cash) basis only:
  - Supplier: s21-5 decreasing adjustment (taxable supply, consideration not received, debt written off as bad OR overdue 12 months or more). Recovery: s21-10 increasing adjustment.
  - Recipient: s21-15 increasing adjustment (creditable acquisition, debt written off by supplier OR overdue 12 months or more, having claimed the credit); repayment: s21-20 decreasing adjustment.
- So a guide that says the 12-month limb applies only to the supplier (or only to the recipient) is wrong. The distinct point: the "written off as bad" limb needs a write-off by the supplier for the supplier's claim; the recipient's adjustment turns on the debt being written off (as known to the recipient) or being overdue 12 months. Cash-basis entities cannot use s21-5 or s21-15 (s21-5(2) states the cash exclusion; the ATO page limits adjustments to non-cash accounting).
- Sources: GSTR 2000/2 paras 8, 13, 21, 22, 31 to 37 (read in full text): https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GST%2FGSTR20002%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001 ; ATO "Types of adjustments" (last updated 2 June 2020) https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/in-detail/managing-gst-in-your-business/reporting-paying-and-activity-statements/making-adjustments-on-your-activity-statements/types-of-adjustments ; Division 21 section headings from the Act TOC https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00446/latest/text/1

### A4. Margin scheme, BAS label G1
- Rule (KNOWN): report the **margin** at G1, not the full price. If margin is nil or negative, report nothing at G1. Report GST on the margin (1/11 of margin) at 1A. ATO worked example: $900,000 sale, $500,000 cost, G1 = $400,000, 1A = $36,363.
- Sources: ATO "Completing your activity statement" (margin scheme; last updated 19 August 2021) https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/in-detail/your-industry/property/gst-and-the-margin-scheme/completing-your-activity-statement ; "Calculating the GST payable" (examples with G1 and 1A) https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/in-detail/your-industry/property/gst-and-the-margin-scheme/calculating-the-gst-payable

### A5. Quarterly BAS: online / agent extra time
- Rule (KNOWN): base due dates Q1 28 Oct, Q2 28 Feb, Q3 28 Apr, Q4 28 Jul. The online two-week concession is **lodge AND pay** ("extra 2 weeks to lodge and pay"), automatic, ongoing, applies to quarters 1, 3 and 4 only (not Q2, which already has 8 weeks). It excludes monthly lodgers, large business clients and others listed on the T&Cs page.
- Q4: 28 July original. Online concession gives 11 August (28 Jul plus 14 days; inferred arithmetic, the concession page does not print the date). So "28 Jul vs 25 Aug" is original vs agent-program date; the self-lodged online date is 11 Aug, not 25 Aug. Weekend or public holiday rolls to next business day.

## Part B: Short-term accommodation

### B1. Commercial residential premises and GSTR 2012/6
- Definition, s195-1 (KNOWN, GSTR 2012/6 para 6): (a) hotel, motel, inn, hostel or boarding house; (b) school accommodation premises; (c) ship mainly let on hire; (d) ship mainly for entertainment or transport; (da) marina with berths occupied by residence ships; (e) caravan park or camping ground; (f) anything similar to (a) to (e). Sale or lease of CRP is taxable; accommodation in CRP provided to an individual by the entity that owns or controls the premises is taxable (para 8). Test is overall impression on physical and operating characteristics (paras 10 to 12): commercial intention, multiple occupancy, holding out to public, accommodation main purpose, central management (reservations, allocating rooms, services), etc.
- Status: a **draft update, GSTR 2012/6DC, was issued 5 Nov 2025** (comments closed 19 Dec 2025), aimed at build-to-rent and clarifying hostels and boarding houses; ATO says the Commissioner's view is unchanged. I could not confirm from the advice-under-development page that it has been finalised as an addendum as of today. Treat GSTR 2012/6 as current and cite the draft as pending.
- Sources: https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GST%2FGSTR20126%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001 ; draft https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=DGC%2FGSTR20126DC1%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001 ; ATO advice under development https://www.ato.gov.au/about-ato/ato-advice-and-guidance/advice-under-development-program/advice-under-development-gst-issues

### B2. Airbnb / Booking.com style lets: input taxed vs taxable
- KNOWN: ATO says rent for residential premises let for residential accommodation is input taxed (no GST charged, no credits). Lease of CRP is taxable. A **single holiday apartment or house is "residential premises" even when let short-term, and even inside a CRP complex**; a lease to a guest or to a manager who runs it as part of CRP is input taxed for the owner (ATO example: owner input taxed, manager's supply taxable). Where an operator owns or leases many units and runs them like a hotel (central management, daily rates, cleaning, linen, reception), the ATO view (private ruling, ATO ID/CRA excerpt) is that premises can be CRP and the accommodation is taxable (registration then needed at $75,000 turnover).
- GSTR 2012/6DC Example 12 (draft, Gus): one apartment let short-term through an on-site manager who acts as agent for several apartments is NOT CRP; the owner's supply is input taxed under para 40-35(1)(a).
- INFERRED: an Adelaide-based manager who holds head leases over many separate apartments/houses and supplies short stays in its own right is at real risk of being a CRP operator (taxable); an agent-only model for individual owners keeps each owner input taxed and the manager's commission taxable. Which one applies depends on the contract structure (agent vs principal), not on the platform. Needs facts.
- Sources: ATO GST and residential property https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/in-detail/your-industry/property/gst-and-residential-property ; Holiday apartments in CRP (updated 21 May 2026) https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/property-and-land/holiday-homes/holiday-apartments-in-commercial-residential-properties ; ATO Commercial residential property https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/in-detail/your-industry/property/gst-and-commercial-property/commercial-residential-property ; ATO ID 1013015866966 (private advice, serviced apartments held to be CRP) https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=EV/1013015866966&PiT=99991231235958

### B3. Division 87 long-term stay concession
- KNOWN: applies only to CRP (commercial accommodation, s87-15). Long-term accommodation = right to occupy for **28 continuous days or more** (s87-20; GSTR 2012/7 para 10). Premises "predominantly for long-term accommodation" = at least 70% of supplies are 28 days or more (s87-20(3); GSTR 2012/7 para 15): value of supply reduced to 50% (s87-5, GSTR 2012/7 para 28). Otherwise s87-10 applies a calculated reduction to the long-term portion. Supplier may choose not to apply Div 87 (s87-25): long-term supplies are then input taxed under para 40-35(1)(b); choice binds at least 12 months. Stays of 27 days or less are always taxable in CRP. Division 87 does not help an operator whose premises are residential (already input taxed).
- Sources: GSTR 2012/7 https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=GST%2FGSTR20127%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001&PiT=20200724000001 ; ATO Commercial residential property page above.

### B4. Who is the supplier when a manager or platform is involved
- KNOWN: GSTR 2012/6DC Example 12: a manager acting as **agent** does not itself supply the accommodation; the owner does. ATO private advice (ID 1013015866966) contrasts entities that own or lease apartments and supply in their own right (taxable CRP operators). GST agent/principal treatment is in Division 153 (Subdivision 153-B allows an intermediary and principal to treat a supply as two supplies by agreement, but LCR 2018/2 para 71 says this is unavailable where an EDP operator is responsible under s84-55).
- Platform: LCR 2018/2 says in most cases the platform is not the supplier; the host or business using the platform is. EDP operator becomes the supplier only under s84-55 (inbound intangible consumer supplies and, for goods, Subdiv 84-C) or by agreement under s84-60 (digital supplies only).
- INFERRED: accommodation in Australian real property is not an inbound intangible consumer supply, so s84-55 does not shift GST liability to Airbnb or Booking.com for Australian stays. I did not find a primary ATO source stating that in terms; treat as inference from the s84-65/84-70 definitions and LCR 2018/2.
- Sources: LCR 2018/2 https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/pdf/pbr/lcr2018-002.pdf ; ATO EDP page https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/international-tax-for-business/gst-for-non-resident-businesses/how-to-charge-gst/if-you-are-an-edp-operator

### B5. GST on Airbnb / Booking.com host fees
- KNOWN (ATO sharing economy and tax, ride-sourcing example generalised by ATO to platform fees): a GST-registered platform may charge GST on service fees or commission; if the host is GST registered they can claim a credit. **If the service is wholly or partly based outside Australia and the host has not given the platform an ABN and a GST-registration declaration, the platform must assume the host is not registered and GST applies.** ATO also says report rental income gross, before platform fees, and that platform fees are deductible.
- INFERRED: a host that only makes input-taxed residential rent cannot claim credits on GST in platform fees; a registered CRP operator can. Offshore-platform reverse charge (s84-5, s84-10) is relevant only where the recipient is registered and the acquisition is not wholly creditable; it does not apply where the platform is itself registered and charges GST. No primary ruling read on Airbnb or Booking.com specifically; registration status of each platform entity was not verified.
- Sources: https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-f47d56cc-0aae-429d-9b35-38375943b40c (Sharing economy and tax) ; https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/sharing-economy-and-tax/renting-out-all-or-part-of-your-home/income-and-deductions-for-renting-out-your-home

### B6. Electronic distribution platform section references (correction)
- KNOWN from the Act TOC: **s84-81 is "Who makes an offshore supply of low value goods"** (Subdiv 84-C), not accommodation. **s84-100 is "When entities are treated as not being Australian consumers"** (Subdiv 84-D, consumers of offshore supplies). The EDP rules sit in Subdiv 84-B: s84-55 (EDP operator treated as supplier), s84-60 (extension by agreement to certain other supplies, digital only, EDP must be registered), s84-65 (inbound intangible consumer supply), s84-70 (meaning of EDP). Low value goods extension is Subdiv 84-C. A guide citing ss84-81 and 84-100 for accommodation platforms is citing the wrong provisions.
- Source: https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00446/latest/text/1 ; LCR 2018/2 paras 48 to 71.

### B7. Sharing Economy Reporting Regime (SERR)
- KNOWN: legislated in Subdivision 396-B, Schedule 1, Taxation Administration Act 1953. **Commenced 1 July 2023 for taxi/ride-sourcing and short-term accommodation**; all other reportable transactions from 1 July 2024. EDP operators report twice yearly: by 31 January (1 Jul to 31 Dec) and 31 July (1 Jan to 30 Jun). Accommodation includes short-term stays such as holiday rentals. Determination 2023: "relevant accommodation" means booked for 90 consecutive days or less; exemptions for multiple-EDP chains and some non-resident suppliers. Reporting is a platform obligation, not a host obligation; ATO uses the data to match income.
- Sources: https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/preparing-lodging-and-paying/third-party-reporting/sharing-economy-reporting-regime/what-is-the-serr ; https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/preparing-lodging-and-paying/third-party-reporting/sharing-economy-reporting-regime/how-and-when-to-report-under-the-serr/transactions-reportable-under-the-serr ; https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2023L01640/asmade/2023-07-01/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html

### B8. Income tax: holiday home deductions (2026 guidance exists)
- KNOWN: new guidance issued 20 May 2026: **TR 2026/1** (rental property income and deductions for individuals not in business), **PCG 2026/2** (apportionment of rental property deductions), **PCG 2026/3** (s26-50 ITAA 1997 applied to holiday homes that are also rented; green/amber/red zones).
  - s26-50 denies deductions for ownership and use expenses of a holiday home (a type of leisure facility: interest, rates, body corporate, repairs, capital works, depreciation) unless the property is used or held for use **mainly** to produce rent (s26-50(3)(b)(ii)), tested objectively across the year. Booking fees, commissions, advertising and cleaning after guests remain deductible.
  - PCG 2026/3: green zone (low risk) = high income-producing occupancy especially in peak periods, limited personal use, commercial terms; amber = increased personal use, forgoing income for personal availability; red = blocking out peak periods for owner, unreasonable restrictions, above-market pricing.
  - PCG 2026/2: time-based method (days used plus days held available on commercial terms, over days in period) and area-based method, combinable. Does not apply where s26-50 fully denies.
  - Transitional: the Commissioner will not devote compliance resources to s26-50 for expenses incurred before 1 July 2026 (TR 2026/1 Appendix 2, para 138). ATO newsroom says the guidance applies to how it assesses from 1 July 2026.
- Relevance: A managed short stay model (a manager letting properties on behalf of owners) is likely fine if each property is mainly rented. Any property the owner or family use materially, or hold blocked for own use, sits at amber/red risk. Bali properties are governed by foreign-source rules and Indonesian law; s26-50 applies to Australian tax on any deduction claimed, not verified for foreign property here.
- Sources: TR 2026/1 https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=TXR%2FTR20261%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001 ; PCG 2026/2 https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=COG%2FPCG20262%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001 ; PCG 2026/3 https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=COG%2FPCG20263%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001 ; ATO Holiday homes page https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/holiday-homes ; newsroom https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-and-super-professionals/for-tax-professionals/tax-professionals-newsroom/new-guidance-for-rental-property-owners

### B9. Division 43 capital works rates, short-term traveller accommodation
- KNOWN (ATO table): building intended on completion to provide short-term accommodation to travellers, in apartment buildings where you own or lease **at least 10 units**, or hotels, motels, guest houses with **at least 10 bedrooms**, construction commenced after 21 August 1979:
  - 22 Aug 1979 to 21 Aug 1984: 2.5%
  - 22 Aug 1984 to 15 Sep 1987: 4%
  - 16 Sep 1987 to 26 Feb 1992: 2.5% (4% where certain pre-16 Sep 1987 contracts)
  - **27 Feb 1992 onwards: 4%** (25 years)
- Ordinary residential or income-producing buildings: construction after 17 Jul 1985; 18 Jul 1985 to 15 Sep 1987 4%; **16 Sep 1987 onwards 2.5%** (40 years). So a single Adelaide apartment or house let short-stay gets 2.5%, not 4%, because the 4% traveller-accommodation category needs the 10-unit or 10-bedroom threshold. (4% vs 2.5% is not about short stay as such.)
- Source: https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/property-and-land/residential-rental-properties/rental-expenses/capital-expenses/work-out-your-capital-works-deductions (as returned by search excerpt; full-page read not completed). Also ATO capital works overview: https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/depreciation-and-capital-expenses-and-allowances/capital-works-deductions . ## Part D: Australia-Indonesia double tax agreement (1992)

Text read from the ATO-hosted synthesised text of the Agreement as modified by the MLI (https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/pdf/mli/indonesia_c1.pdf), the Indonesian Tax Office consolidated text, and the tabled treaty text (ParlInfo). The 1992 Agreement is scheduled to the International Tax Agreements Act 1953; I did not fetch the Schedule from legislation.gov.au (blocked to WebFetch and not searched further). Explanatory memorandum: https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=NEM/EM92018/NAT/ATO/00004&PiT=99991231235958

### D1. Residence tie-breaker (Article 4(3), individuals)
- Applies only if the person is a resident of both countries under each domestic law (art 4(1)); a person liable to tax in a State only on source-based income is not a resident (art 4(2)).
- Order: (a) permanent home available; (b) if in both or neither, habitual abode; (c) if in both or neither, closer **economic and personal relations**. There is no nationality or mutual agreement step for individuals (unlike OECD model), and no "centre of vital interests" wording (the third test is "economic and personal relations are closer"). Companies: Article 4(4) place of effective management, replaced by MLI art 4 (competent authority agreement, effective management, place of incorporation).
- Relevance: a hypothetical resident individual who owns a home in Adelaide and a villa in Bali (a home available in both) would move to habitual abode, then economic and personal relations.

### D2. Key articles for time split Adelaide and Bali
- **Business profits, art 7**: taxable only in the residence State unless the enterprise carries on business in the other State through a PE, in which case the other State may tax profits attributable to the PE and also to sales of the same or similar goods or business activities of the same or similar kind ("force of attraction" for sales and similar activities, art 7(1)(b) and (c)). PE (art 5) is a fixed place of business, plus deemed PE for building sites over 120 days and service furnishing (including consultancy) through employees for more than 120 days aggregate in 12 months on the same or a connected project.
- **Independent personal services, art 14**: income of an individual resident of one State taxable only there unless (a) a fixed base is regularly available in the other State, or (b) the individual is present in the other State more than **120 days in any 12 months** (then only income from activities there). Note the 120-day threshold, not 183.
- **Dependent personal services (employment), art 15**: taxable in the residence State unless the employment is exercised in the other State, where that State may tax the remuneration from that exercise; exemption (art 15(2)) needs all four: present not more than **120 days aggregate in any 12 months**, employer not a resident of the work State, remuneration not borne by a PE or fixed base there, and remuneration taxed in residence State.
- **Dividends, art 10**: source-State tax capped at **15%** of gross; Australia charges 15% only on unfranked dividends to non-residents (EM); franked dividends stay free under domestic law. Branch profits tax cap 15% (art 10(6)). Art 10(5) exempts dividends paid by a company resident in one State to a non-resident of the other from tax by that other State (extra-territorial), except where effectively connected.
- **Interest, art 11**: source-State cap **10%** of gross.
- **Royalties, art 12**: **10%** for equipment rentals and know-how/information supply categories (arts 12(3)(b), (c), and related (d), (f)); **15%** for all other royalties (copyright, patents, trademarks, films etc.). A property or PE that carries the income overrides these caps (art 7 or 14 applies).
- **Real property income, art 6**: taxed where property is situated (Bali villa rent taxable in Indonesia; Australia taxes residents on worldwide income with credit under art 24).
- **Double tax relief, art 24**: Australia gives foreign tax credit for Indonesian tax paid per the Agreement (Indonesian source per art 23).
- **MLI**: the Agreement is modified by the MLI as shown in the ATO synthesised text (art 4(4) company residence, PE splitting-up of contracts art 14, closely related persons art 15, saving clause art 11 MLI: the Agreement does not restrict a State taxing its own residents except for listed articles).
- Caveats: Indonesian domestic law (183-day resident test, withholding rates, VAT, PMA/foreign-owned company rules) not verified here and out of scope of the ask. Indonesian withholding on dividends, interest and royalties under domestic law is 20% (not verified); treaty rates only apply where Indonesian formalities (DGT form) are met (not verified).
