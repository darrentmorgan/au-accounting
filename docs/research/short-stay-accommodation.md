## 0. Bottom line

1. A house, unit, granny flat or room let to travellers by an individual is **residential premises**, and the rent is **input taxed** whatever the length of stay (GSTA 1999 s 40-35(1)(a), (2)(a); the definition in s 195-1 applies "regardless of the term of occupation"). No GST on the rent, no input tax credits on the costs, and the rent does not count towards GST turnover. [K]
2. Short-stay income is taxable **only** if the premises are **commercial residential premises** (CRP: hotel, motel, inn, hostel, boarding house or anything similar; s 195-1), decided by overall impression using the GSTR 2012/6 indicia. Small-scale hosting (two rooms in a house; one strata apartment let through an agent-manager) is residential. A hosted three-room B&B with daily cleaning, breakfast and a reception is CRP in the ruling's own example. Grey cases are escalated, never decided. [K]
3. Registration turns on carrying on an enterprise and GST turnover meeting the threshold (s 23-5, s 23-15 and the regulation amount; ATO page states the current amount and the 21-day deadline). Input taxed sales are excluded from GST turnover (s 188-15(1)(a)). [K]
4. Division 87 (long-term stays, 28 continuous days or more) helps **only** CRP operators. It does nothing for residential premises, which are already input taxed. [K]
5. Income tax: the ATO's current position is TR 2026/1 (rental property income and deductions for individuals not in business), PCG 2026/2 (apportionment) and PCG 2026/3 (s 26-50 holiday homes), all issued 20 May 2026. They cover short-term letting expressly. [K]
6. Direct letting costs (platform commission, agent fees, advertising, cleaning after guest stays) are deductible in full. Ownership and use costs (interest, rates, water, land tax, body corporate, insurance, repairs, depreciation, capital works) are apportioned by days available on commercial terms and, for part of a home, by floor area. If the property is a **holiday home** (owner, family or friends use it for holidays free or at a reduced rate) and is not used or held mainly to produce rent, the ownership and use costs are denied outright (ITAA 1997 s 26-50). [K]
7. Sharing Economy Reporting Regime (TAA 1953 Sch 1 Subdiv 396-B, s 396-55 table item 15): platforms report short-term accommodation payments to the ATO from 1 Jul 2023 (all other sharing economy transactions from 1 Jul 2024), twice a year. The report carries the host's identity, bank details, property address, nights, gross income including GST, and fees withheld. The ATO can see gross, so a host who declares net-of-fees income is visibly under-reporting. SERR is a reporting regime only: item 15 applies where no withholding is required, and the ATO warns hosts that platform income may reach them with no tax withheld. [K]
8. From the 2027-28 income year the residential dwelling loss quarantine (ITAA 1997 ss 26-155, 26-160, Act 49 of 2026) is expected to catch a short-stay house or unit (the Explanatory Memorandum says short-stay apartments can be residential dwellings) unless the interest was last acquired before 7.30 pm ACT time on 12 May 2026 or another exception applies. [K text; I on application to any given host]
9. Entities (company, trust, partnership, SMSF), businesses, many properties, hotel-like operations, strata hotel pools, head-lease operators and foreign-resident owners leave the individual passive-investor model and are escalated.

### 3.2 The hotel-like test (GSTR 2012/6)

- Whether premises are CRP is a question of fact, "impression and degree", weighed over the premises as a whole, not one room (paras 11, 25, 41). [K]
- Objective evidence: physical character and how the premises are run; if unclear, contracts and planning or zoning permissions (para 10). [K]
- Characteristics of operating hotels and similar premises that are relevant but not decisive (para 12): commercial or business-like operation; capacity for several unrelated guests at once; held out to the public; accommodation is the main purpose; central management (reservations, allocating rooms, taking payment, arranging services, on or off site); the operator supplies in its own right, not as agent; services or facilities provided or arranged; occupants are guests (travellers with a home elsewhere, no tenant-like exclusive right). [K]
- Features pointing away from CRP (para 41, absence does not prove CRP, presence of some does not prove not): periodic lease term, written condition report, cleaning fee on exit, alteration and pets allowed, occupant arranges utilities and does own cleaning, unfurnished, loan-for-occupancy. [K]
- Worked examples inside the ruling that the eval-author can rely on (each is the ATO's own conclusion): B&B with three bedrooms, communal dining, on-site owner, daily cleaning, breakfast: CRP, taxable (Example 2, paras 49 to 50). House with two furnished spare rooms, linen only: not CRP, input taxed (Example 3, paras 51 to 52). Farm stay suites with daily cleaning, on-site manager, in own right: CRP (Example 4, paras 53 to 55). One strata holiday apartment let through an on-site manager who is agent for several apartments: not CRP, owner's supply input taxed (Example 12, paras 82 to 85). [K]
- Separately titled units: rooms or apartments combined with enough commercial infrastructure (reception, dining, function rooms, laundry, car parking) can be run as CRP (paras 95 to 97). But a strata unit sold or leased on its own is characterised on its own: without that infrastructure it is residential regardless of how the building operates (para 98, Example 16 paras 102 to 107). So the **owner's lease** of an apartment to a hotel operator is input taxed, while the **operator's supplies to guests** are taxable CRP accommodation. [K]
- Court authority on the same split: the Full Federal Court in the South Steyne litigation held apartment leases to the hotel operator were input taxed residential premises, and the room supplied to a guest was a taxable supply of CRP accommodation made by the operator as principal (summarised in MBI Properties v FCT [2013] FCA 56). The High Court later decided a related Division 135 question (Commissioner v MBI Properties [2014] HCA 49). [K via case summaries] https://www4.austlii.edu.au/au/cases/cth/FCA/2013/56.html ; https://vvv.austlii.edu.au/au/cases/cth/HCA/2014/49.html
- A Tribunal decision on an Airbnb host renting rooms in her own four-bedroom home with continental breakfast found she was not carrying on a business and was not supplying CRP. TR 2026/1 footnote 4 cites it. Not binding precedent, but a useful fact pattern. [K] https://www7.austlii.edu.au/cgi-bin/viewdoc/au/cases/cth/AATA/2021/4844.html
- Status of update: draft GSTR 2012/6DC (5 Nov 2025) targets build-to-rent, hostels and boarding houses; the ATO says its view is unchanged; not final as at 7 Sep 2026. [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=DGC%2FGSTR20126DC1%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001
- Illustration only (do not rely, archived): a 2016 ATO private advice treated an entity operating many leased or owned apartments across sites, with daily cleaning, linen and central operations, as supplying CRP accommodation. [K, weak] https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=EV/1013015866966&PiT=99991231235958

### 3.3 Who is the supplier

- Agent model: where a manager supplies as **agent** for the owner, the owner makes the supply of accommodation and the manager does not. For hotels the ruling says the arrangement documents show whether an agent-principal relationship exists (GSTR 2012/6 paras 24, 83 to 84). An agent that only facilitates accommodation sales is not responsible for GST on the accommodation but may have GST obligations on its own services, so the manager's fee is a separate supply to the owner. [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/international-tax-for-business/gst-for-non-resident-businesses/gst-on-sales-of-australian-accommodation-by-offshore-sellers
- Head-lease or principal model (operator holds the leases or licences and sells stays in its own name): the owner's lease is input taxed; the operator's guest supplies may be CRP. Whether they are turns on facts and contracts: escalate (AU-GST-002). [K on the split; I on any given operator]
- Platforms: in most cases the supplier is the host or business using the platform, not the platform. A platform that acts only as agent on its own site is not an EDP for SERR purposes. [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/preparing-lodging-and-paying/third-party-reporting/sharing-economy-reporting-regime/how-and-when-to-report-under-the-serr/transactions-reportable-under-the-serr ; https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/preparing-lodging-and-paying/third-party-reporting/sharing-economy-reporting-regime/who-needs-to-report-under-the-serr
- A single apartment inside a CRP complex, leased to a guest or to a management company that runs it as part of the complex, remains an input taxed lease for the owner (ATO worked example, owner "Aiko"). [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/property-and-land/holiday-homes/holiday-apartments-in-commercial-residential-properties

### 3.5 Registration and GST turnover interaction

- You must be registered if you carry on an enterprise and your GST turnover meets the registration turnover threshold (s 23-5). The Act's base amount was raised by regulation; the ATO page states the current threshold and that you register within 21 days of reaching it (existing keys `gst.registration_threshold`, `gst.registration_threshold_nfp`, `gst.registration_deadline_days`). [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=PAC/19990055/23-5&PiT=99991231235958 ; https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=PAC/19990055/23-15&PiT=99991231235958 ; https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/registering-for-gst
- Current GST turnover is the sum of values of supplies made or likely over the 12 months, **excluding input taxed supplies**, supplies not for consideration, and supplies not made in an enterprise you carry on (s 188-15(1)). So residential short-stay rent, however large, does not count. The ATO page describes projected turnover as the current month plus the next 11 months; I did not read s 188-20, so that the same exclusions apply to projected turnover is [I]. [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=PAC/19990055/188-15&PiT=99991231235958
- Other taxable income counts: consulting, management fees charged to other owners, taxable sales of CRP accommodation, separately charged taxable extras. A host who is below the threshold on those alone is not required to register. [K on rule; I on any given fact pattern]
- Offshore sellers of Australian **commercial** accommodation must count those sales toward the threshold; residential-style short-stay lettings do not attract GST. [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/international-tax-for-business/gst-for-non-resident-businesses/gst-on-sales-of-australian-accommodation-by-offshore-sellers
- A registered owner still makes input taxed supplies when letting residential premises; registration does not change the classification (s 9-30(2), s 40-35). [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=PAC/19990055/9-30&PiT=99991231235958
- Sharing-economy rule of thumb on the ATO's asset and service pages: registration is needed where GST turnover reaches the threshold (or for fuel tax credits); ride-sourcing is the regardless-of-turnover case. [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/sharing-economy-and-tax/sharing-assets-excluding-accommodation/how-gst-applies-when-sharing-assets ; https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-and-super-professionals/for-tax-professionals/prepare-and-lodge/tax-time/before-you-lodge/sharing-economy

### 3.6 Credits and costs

- No input tax credit for acquisitions to the extent they relate to making input taxed supplies (s 11-15(2)(a)); the ATO says the same for holiday apartments and for residential rent generally. Management fees, cleaning and platform fees the host pays are costs, not credits. [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=PAC/19990055/11-15&PiT=99991231235958 ; https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/in-detail/your-industry/property/gst-and-residential-property
- A registered CRP operator claims credits on its costs and pays GST on taxable accommodation at one-eleventh of the GST-inclusive price (ATO CRP page, short-term stays under 28 days). [K]
- A person who makes both taxable supplies and input taxed rent needs a documented apportionment for shared overheads (AU-GST-003 exists). [K on rule]
- Bond or security deposit for residential premises: no GST (ATO residential property page). [K] Retained bonds are still assessable income (see 4.1).

### 3.7 GST traps

1. Confusing "short stay" with "hotel". Length of stay never changes residential premises into CRP. [K]
2. Charging or claiming GST because the platform shows a GST line on its fee. The host's credit rule is s 11-15(2)(a). [K]
3. Counting residential rent in GST turnover. [K]
4. Applying Division 87 to a normal Airbnb house. [K]
5. Counting 28 days wrong: check-in 1 March and check-out 28 March is 27 days; check-out 29 March is 28 days (s 87-20(2)). [K]
6. Treating an agent-manager's fee as GST-free or claiming it. [K on classification; the manager's registration status is a fact]
7. Serviced apartments in a hotel pool: owner residential, operator taxable. [K]
8. A host who adds hotel-like services (daily cleaning, breakfast, reception, central booking for several dwellings) can drift into CRP. Say the tests; refuse the determination. [K]
9. Offshore hosts of Australian commercial accommodation counting toward turnover. [K]

## 4. Income tax

### 4.1 Assessable income

- Rent, licence fees and similar charges from a rental property are assessable, whether received directly, through an agent or through an online platform, and **even if below market** (TR 2026/1 paras 20 to 21, 49 to 50, 65 to 68). [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=TXR%2FTR20261%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001
- Declare income **before** platform fees and commissions, including host or cleaning fees charged to guests. Also include insurance payouts (for example for damage or lost rent), bonds or deposits you become entitled to keep, and booking, letting and cancellation fees you retain. [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/sharing-economy-and-tax/renting-out-all-or-part-of-your-home/income-and-deductions-for-renting-out-your-home ; https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/property-and-land/residential-rental-properties/rental-income-you-must-declare
- Year of income: rent is declared in the year the tenant pays; amounts paid to an agent or manager count when the agent gets them, not when they reach you. Whether a platform holding funds until after check-in is treated as your agent for timing is not addressed. [K; platform timing U] same rental-income URL
- Co-owners share income and expenses by legal title (joint tenants equally, tenants in common by deed shares), except in very limited circumstances of proven different equitable interests (TR 2026/1 paras 22, 51 to 53; TR 93/32). [K]
- Money received from household members that is only a share of household costs or care is not income; sub-letting or a licence to occupy is (TR 2026/1 paras 23 to 24, 55 to 64). A student homestay payment splits into a non-assessable household-cost part and an assessable rent part (Examples 4 to 5). [K]
- Providing services such as breakfast or cleaning with a room does not make it "board" or anything other than renting your space (ATO). [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/sharing-economy-and-tax/renting-out-all-or-part-of-your-home

### 4.2 Investor or business

- Renting property, including on a platform, does not of itself mean carrying on a business (TR 2026/1 para 6). The ATO says most owners are investors, including owners of several properties; business indicators are size and scale, hours spent, extensive personal involvement and business-like organisation, using the eight indicators in TR 97/11 (para 13). [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/property-and-land/residential-rental-properties/rental-property-as-investment-or-business
- TR 2026/1, PCG 2026/2 and PCG 2026/3 expressly do **not** cover individuals using rental property in a business or entities that are not individuals (TR 2026/1 para 5; PCG 2026/2 para 5; PCG 2026/3 para 10). So business-scale or entity-owned short-stay is outside the individual model. [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=COG%2FPCG20262%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001 ; https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=COG%2FPCG20263%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001
- Being in business changes several rules: s 26-31 travel has a business exception, s 40-27 second-hand asset denial has a business exception, and s 26-50(3)(b)(i) has a business exception (leisure facilities used in a business of providing leisure facilities for payment). Escalate rather than compute. [K on the exceptions] see 4.7 and 4.5.

### 4.4 Available for rent on commercial terms, and blocked-out periods

- A rental property that is not a holiday home is deductible because it is rented or **held to produce income**, meaning available for rent on commercial terms. Indicators for: broad advertising exposure, rent comparable to similar properties, requests actively monitored. Indicators against: advertised only at work, by word of mouth or restricted groups; blocked out for you, family or friends at below-market rates during likely demand; unlikely to attract renters because of location, condition or access; unreasonable conditions (rent above comparables, references for short holiday stays, "no children", parts of the property inaccessible); requests not monitored or refused without adequate reason. [K] PCG 2026/2 paras 16 to 17; https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/property-and-land/residential-rental-properties/deductions-for-a-rental-property-that-isnt-a-holiday-home
- If those against-factors dominate, the ATO says the property is generally not held to produce income and has no deduction for its expenses that year. The ATO example (an Adelaide owner who sets rent above comparables, rejects applicants and refuses agent advice) is a total denial, not an apportionment. [K] same URL
- Days the property is blocked out for private use are **not** "days held to produce income". A room in your own home has **zero** held days: on days it is not rented it is private, even if the listing stays live (PCG 2026/2 paras 15, 41; ATO worked example with 86 days rented, 279 days not available). [K]
- Days for which rent is paid count as used to produce income even if nobody stays (PCG 2026/2 para 14). [K]
- Days a property is off the market for repairs or renovation are not addressed by the ATO material read. [U] Ask for evidence and flag.
- Booking calendars, price history and enquiry response records are the evidence (PCG 2026/2 para 2: keep records supporting how the Guideline applies). [K on the record duty; I on which documents] 

### 4.5 Holiday homes (ITAA 1997 s 26-50)

Rules:
- S1. "Leisure facility": land, a building or part of one, used or held for use for holidays or recreation. A "holiday home" (ATO's term) is such a property used or held for use for your holidays or recreation, **or the holidays or recreation of family or friends for no rent or reduced rent**. [K] s 26-50(2) https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=PAC/19970038/26-50&PiT=99991231235958 ; TR 2026/1 para 12
- S2. s 26-50(1) denies losses or outgoings to the extent incurred to acquire or retain ownership, acquire or retain rights of use, use, operate, maintain or repair the facility, or in relation to obligations of ownership or rights of use. [K]
- S3. Exception (s 26-50(3)(b)(ii)): no denial if at all times in the income year you use it, or hold it for use, **mainly** to produce rent, licence fees or similar. "Mainly" means chiefly or for the most part and is tested objectively and qualitatively, not by counting days alone and not by your intention (TR 2026/1 paras 37 to 43, 104 to 114). Factors: actual use; time dedicated to income use; time used or held for private use; how available or used as a rental when holiday demand peaks (school holidays, public holidays, seasonal peaks). A property advertised more than half the year but unavailable or unused for rent in the desirable periods points to not mainly rental (para 112). Peak periods are location-specific (para 113). [K]
- S4. Even when the exception applies, ownership and use costs are still apportioned for private use (TR 2026/1 para 103 and Example 11 para 118; PCG 2026/3 Examples 1 to 4). [K]
- S5. Part-year exception (s 26-50(4)): needs a definite and sustained change in the pattern of use during the year (for example the owner moves overseas and lifts all restrictions). A one-off different use or a regular seasonal pattern is not a change (TR 2026/1 paras 46 to 47, 127 to 135). [K]
- S6. Anti-avoidance (s 26-50(7)) can override the exception where a scheme was entered into to get within it (TR 2026/1 paras 44 to 45). [K]
- S7. A property is not a holiday home if you never use or reserve it for your own or friends' holidays and take all reasonable steps to rent it (TR 2026/1 Example 10; ATO Cho example). [K]
- S8. Denied costs may form part of the cost base of the property for CGT (TR 2026/1 para 95, fn 51; ATO records page tells owners to keep records of costs they could not deduct). [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/property-and-land/residential-rental-properties/records-for-rental-properties-and-holiday-homes
- S9. Transitional: the ATO will not devote compliance resources to whether s 26-50 applies to holiday home expenses **incurred before 1 July 2026** (TR 2026/1 para 138; PCG 2026/3 paras 12 to 13). The section itself is unchanged, and the ATO will still state the same view in any private ruling or assessment (para 140). The existing key is `rental.holiday_home_compliance_start`. So for 2025-26 the law denies but the ATO is not reviewing it; from 1 Jul 2026 (mostly 2026-27) it is reviewing. Do not tell a client in 2025-26 that the rule "does not apply". [K]

PCG 2026/3 compliance zones (qualitative, no day-count thresholds; do not invent any):

| Zone | Behaviour (paraphrased) | ATO approach |
|---|---|---|
| Green | High income-producing occupancy, especially at desirable times; little personal use; income prioritised; commercial terms; actively maximising income | No compliance resources except to confirm the facts |
| Amber | More personal use by you and friends at no cost or below market; forgoing income to keep it available for yourself; personal use at peak times; limited attempts to rent | May apply compliance resources |
| Red | Blocking out times each year for personal use, particularly peaks; limited attempts to rent; major features inaccessible; unreasonable restrictions; no effort to lift occupancy; pricing above market | Priority attention, may audit |

PCG 2026/3 examples worth knowing (all ATO conclusions): a Gold Coast apartment in a rental pool with four weeks blocked in low demand: green (Ex 1); a Barossa house with one week owner use in the 4-month peak, otherwise actively managed: green (Ex 2); a Melbourne CBD apartment used by Adelaide owners for major sporting events: amber (Ex 6); a Sorrento house rented 3 to 4 weeks, owner's wine collection, boat and jet-ski inaccessible: red and denied (Ex 8); a Busselton house always blocked for Christmas, Easter and summer holidays, agent instructed not to let, many enquiries rejected, about 10 weeks rented: red (Ex 9). [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=COG%2FPCG20263%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001 ; ATO holiday homes page.

### 4.6 Apportionment (PCG 2026/2)

Time-based method (paras 13 to 15, formula in the ATO's image alt text and repeated on the ATO "Renting out part of a home" page):

`deductible share = (days used to produce income + days held to produce income) / days in the income year you owned the property`

- days used to produce income: nights occupied for rent, plus days paid even if unoccupied. days held: unoccupied nights where the property is available for rent on commercial terms. [K]
- Denominator is the number of days in the income year the owner owned the property: 365 for 2025-26 and 2026-27; **366 for 2027-28** (includes 29 Feb 2028); part-year ownership uses days owned. [K on formula; calendar arithmetic mine]
- Change in use during the year (property first let, or stops being let): the same formula applied across the periods (Example 4: 243 of 365 days). [K]

Area-based method (paras 29 to 32):

`area share = (A + B/2) / C`, A = floor area solely used by the guest or tenant, B = shared common areas, C = whole property. Half the common areas is the fixed convention. [K]

Combined (paras 33 to 43): multiply the two shares when part of a home is let for part of the year. Room example: (10 + 50/2) / 80 = 43.75%; 100/365; product 11.99% (PCG) . ATO web example (Jane) uses the same method. [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/sharing-economy-and-tax/renting-out-all-or-part-of-your-home/renting-out-part-of-a-home

Whole main residence let occasionally (owner away during bookings): time-based only, on nights let, and direct costs 100% (ATO John and Mary example). [K]

Family and friends:
- Market-rate rent to family or friends: no apportionment if you can evidence the market rate as set at the time (valuation or comparable listings) (PCG 2026/2 paras 47 to 49). [K]
- Below-market rent: mixed use; apportion out the private element, and where deductions would exceed rent received the ATO accepts limiting them to rent received, giving a nil result (paras 44 to 46, Example 7). [K]
- Rent-free or reduced-rate stays by family or friends also make it a **holiday home** under TR 2026/1 para 12, so the s 26-50 mainly-for-rent test applies (Example 12, para 121, says a similar outcome arises if friends stay rent-free or below market). [K]
- PCG 2026/2 gives no day-level method for occasional discounted short stays by friends. Conservative treatment: count those nights as private (excluded from the numerator) and flag. [I]

Rounding in the ATO's own examples: percentages shown to two decimals, dollars truncated (for example 335/365 x $34,801 = $31,940.64 shown as $31,940; Jane's 17.98% of $26,153 shown as $4,702). Graders should tolerate a dollar. [K]

### 4.7 Other rules that bite short-stay hosts

- **Travel (s 26-31):** no deduction for travel expense incurred to earn income from using residential premises as residential accommodation, unless necessarily incurred in carrying on a business (property investing or property management services qualify) or the taxpayer is an excluded entity (corporate tax entity, non-SMSF super plan, MIT, public unit trust, or unit trust or partnership made up only of those). Applies to trips to clean, hand over keys, inspect, collect rent or repair. Paying a cleaner or manager is still deductible. [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=PAC/19970038/26-31&PiT=99991231235958 ; LCR 2018/7
- **Second-hand assets (s 40-27):** decline in value on a depreciating asset used to earn residential-accommodation income, not in a business, is denied where you did not hold the asset when it was first used (second-hand), or it was used in your residence or for a private non-occasional purpose; excluded entities are exempt (corporate tax entities and others). New furnishings are fine. The 2017 cut-off is the existing `rental.second_hand_asset_*` keys. [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=PAC/19970038/40-27&PiT=99991231235958
- **Capital works (Div 43):** a single house or apartment gets the ordinary residential rate for works begun from 16 Sep 1987. The higher traveller-accommodation rate needs at least 10 units or 10 bedrooms (existing keys `rental.capital_works_rate_residential`, `rental.capital_works_rate_traveller_accommodation`, `rental.capital_works_traveller_minimum_units`). Capital works are among the costs s 26-50 denies. [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/property-and-land/residential-rental-properties/rental-expenses/capital-expenses/work-out-your-capital-works-deductions
- **Repairs vs improvements:** TR 97/23 remains the repairs ruling. [K] (cited in TR 2026/1 fn 7)
- **Land tax timing:** land tax is incurred when the liability arises under state law, not when assessed or paid (TR 2026/1 para 71), and it is an ownership cost, so it is subject to s 26-50 and apportionment. [K]

### 4.8 Owners other than individuals

TR 2026/1, PCG 2026/2 and PCG 2026/3 do not apply to entities (see 4.2). s 26-31(2) and s 40-27(3) carve out corporate tax entities and certain trusts and partnerships, so for a company the travel and second-hand asset rules differ. s 26-50 speaks of "you" and has no entity exclusion, so I read it as applying to any taxpayer [I]. Likewise s 26-155(1) speaks of "you" with entity exceptions only for widely held unit trusts and complying super entities, so the 2027-28 quarantine is not limited to individuals [I from the Act text]. Private use of an entity-owned holiday property by owners or associates raises other regimes (Division 7A, FBT, trust distribution rules). Escalate (AU-RENT-001); do not compute. [K on carve-outs]

### 4.9 Residential dwelling loss quarantine from 2027-28 (preview only)

- ITAA 1997 s 26-155 (inserted by Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026, No. 49 of 2026, Sch 2): deductions relating to using or holding residential dwellings as residential accommodation, in excess of assessable income from them, are not deductible in the year, and are carried forward and can only be applied against future income from residential dwellings, revenue gains or residential capital gains. The EM (paras 2.20 to 2.21) says there is no restriction based on the length of time the dwelling can be occupied and gives short-stay apartments as an example of something that can still be a residential dwelling, and that a hotel-type property is excluded. [K] https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/search/display/display.w3p;query=Id%3A%22legislation%2Fems%2Fr7493_ems_a90ad43e-17d7-4cd3-859b-84ac4e6f3dea%22

### 5.3 What is not reported (exemptions in LI 2025/5)

- Where two platforms are in the chain and the other one reports, the first can be exempt after notifying the ATO (s 6). [K]
- Supplier is a listed entity or its wholly owned subsidiary, or a government body (s 7(1)). [K]
- Supplier is a "substantial supplier": at least $1,000,000 (GST inclusive) of supplies facilitated by that platform in the 12 months to the end of the period (prorated for new suppliers) (ss 4, 7(2)). [K]
- The property is a "substantial property": at least 2,000 platform transactions in 12 months (prorated) (ss 4, 8(a)). The ATO page describes this as a CRP accommodation property. [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/preparing-lodging-and-paying/third-party-reporting/sharing-economy-reporting-regime/how-and-when-to-report-under-the-serr/transactions-exempt-from-reporting-under-the-serr
- Platform is itself treated as the supplier under GST Act s 84-55 (inbound intangible consumer supplies) (s 7(3)); not accommodation in Australia. [K]
- Supplier has no Australian address, service not provided in Australia, payment not to an Australian account and no other sign of Australian residence, all four together (s 8(b)). [K]
- A booking with no price and no payment through the platform, where the operator has no visibility (s 8(c)). [K]
- So "no platform report" does not mean "no income". Large operators, hotel-scale properties and booking-only intermediaries may not be reported. [I]
- Foreign-resident host of an **Australian** property: the service is provided in Australia, so the s 8(b) exemption fails and the booking is reportable. [I] An Australian-resident host of an overseas property (Bali): the supplier is in Australia, but the BIG requires property country AU; whether such bookings are reported is [U] (Open question O3).

### 5.4 What it means for a host

1. The ATO holds a per-property, per-period record of gross receipts, fees and nights. Declare gross before fees, deduct the fees separately, and reconcile to the platform's annual or half-year statements (Example C). [K]
2. Where a manager or agent holds the listing, the reported seller may be the manager rather than the owner; the owner still declares gross rent from the manager's statements (ATO: report gross rent before management fees). [I on who is reported; K on the gross rule] rental-income URL in 4.1.
3. The ATO's earlier accommodation data-matching program (formal notices under TAA Sch 1 s 353-10) obtained per-listing data including owner block-out dates, price per night, minimum-night rules, enquiry and conversion rates and cancellations for 2016-17 to 2019-20. Its existence shows the ATO can test the "available on commercial terms" evidence, not only the income. [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/about-ato/commitments-and-reporting/in-detail/privacy-and-information-gathering/how-we-use-data-matching/sharing-economy-accommodation-data-matching-program-protocol/sharing-economy-accommodation-data
4. SERR does not withhold tax or register anyone for GST. [K] (4.10, 3.5)
5. Hosts should assume every platform booking is visible, including bookings that were refunded or cancelled after payment (the report nets refunds only for cancellations through the platform). [K on field; I on advice]

## 6. CGT (orientation; the `cgt` skill owns it)

- Renting out part of a home, or all of it while you are away, can reduce the main residence exemption. The test is whether interest on money borrowed to buy the home would be deductible in whole or part (s 118-190(1)(c)), and the partial exemption reflects floor area used and the time it was used to produce assessable income. [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=PAC/19970038/118-190&PiT=99991231235958 ; https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/capital-gains-tax/property-and-capital-gains-tax/your-main-residence-home/using-your-home-for-rental-or-business ; https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/sharing-economy-and-tax/renting-out-all-or-part-of-your-home/capital-gains-tax-when-renting-out-accommodation
- If the home is first used to produce income after 20 Aug 1996 and would have been fully exempt just before that time, its cost base is reset to market value at first income use (s 118-192). Ask for a valuation; never estimate one. [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=PAC/19970038/118-192&PiT=99991231235958
- ATO method (own steps in Example I): gain measured from that market value; multiply by floor area share; multiply by days used for income divided by days from first income use to sale. Days are counted inclusively (the ATO example counts 1 Jul 2019 to 30 Jun 2025 as 2,192 days). [K]
- A foreign resident at the sale contract date generally has no main residence exemption (ATO). [K] https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/capital-gains-tax/property-and-capital-gains-tax/your-main-residence-home/using-your-home-for-rental-or-business
- Holiday home costs denied by s 26-50 may be added to the cost base (TR 2026/1 para 95). [K]
- TR 2026/1 para 9 flags that renting part or all of the main residence can compromise the exemption and points to IT 2673 and TD 1999/71. [K]
- Discount and post-1 July 2027 rules (indexation, minimum tax, quarantined loss offset) belong to `cgt`; do not restate figures.

## 10. Worked examples (all figures synthetic; computed step by step from the rules above)

All arithmetic was done by hand and cross-checked with a throwaway script outside the repo. Do not derive expected values by running the repo calculators.

### Example A: whole house let short-stay by an individual (2026-27, 365 days)

Facts: Victor Harbor house, individual owner, not in business, owned all year. Nights: 225 rented; 119 unoccupied but advertised on two platforms at prices comparable with similar properties, with enquiries monitored; 14 owner nights and 7 nights for friends staying free, all off-peak and unbooked. 225 + 119 + 14 + 7 = 365. Gross rent before fees: 225 nights x $240 = $54,000. Direct costs: platform commission $6,480; cleaning after guest stays $9,000. Ownership costs: interest $21,600; council rates $2,400; water $1,100; insurance $1,900; repairs $1,000; capital works on $240,000 of residential construction begun in 2005 at 2.5% = $6,000. Ownership total $34,000.

1. Holiday home? Yes, the owner and friends use it for holidays free. So s 26-50 must be tested.
2. Mainly for rent? Assume the facts are green zone: 21 private nights of 365, all off-peak, commercial pricing, monitored requests. Exception s 26-50(3)(b)(ii) applies for the whole year. State the assumption; if the facts were amber or red the result differs (step 8).
3. Direct costs deductible in full: $6,480 + $9,000 = $15,480.
4. Time share = (225 + 119) / 365 = 344 / 365 = 94.2466%. The 21 private nights are excluded.
5. Deductible ownership costs = $34,000 x 344 / 365 = $32,043.84. Private portion $34,000 - $32,043.84 = $1,956.16.
6. Total deductions = $32,043.84 + $15,480 = $47,523.84.
7. Net rental result = $54,000 - $47,523.84 = **net rental income $6,476.16**.
8. Contrast (red zone): the owner blocks Christmas, Easter and school holidays and the house is rented 60 nights (60 x $240 = $14,400 gross; commission $1,728; cleaning $2,400). s 26-50(1) denies the whole $34,000; no apportionment. Deductions = $1,728 + $2,400 = $4,128; net rental income $10,272 and no claim for interest, rates or depreciation (costs may go to the cost base).
9. Year-boundary: with the same day counts in 2027-28 the denominator is 366: $34,000 x 344 / 366 = $31,956.28 (income year has 29 Feb 2028). In 2025-26 the law is the same but the ATO is not reviewing s 26-50 for expenses incurred before 1 Jul 2026 (TR 2026/1 para 138).
Sources: PCG 2026/2 paras 10 to 17; TR 2026/1 paras 12, 37 to 43, 138; PCG 2026/3 Table 1 and Example 1; s 26-50.

### Example B: spare room in the owner's home (2026-27)

Facts: 120 m2 home, spare bedroom 14 m2, shared areas guests use (kitchen, bathroom, lounge, deck) 46 m2. Room let 128 nights; gross $180 per night = $23,040. Ownership costs (interest $15,200; rates $2,100; insurance $1,300; power, water and internet $3,400) = $22,000. Direct: platform commission $1,050; guest laundry and cleaning $1,280.
1. Area share = (14 + 46/2) / 120 = 37/120 = 30.8333%.
2. Time share: days held = 0 for a room in your own home, so 128 / 365 = 35.0685%.
3. Combined = 37/120 x 128/365 = 4,736 / 43,800 = 10.8128%.
4. Deductible ownership costs = $22,000 x 10.8128% = $2,378.81.
5. Direct costs $1,050 + $1,280 = $2,330.
6. Total deductions $4,708.81; net rental income $23,040 - $4,708.81 = **$18,331.19**.
A room in your own home is not a holiday home (never used for the owner's holidays), so s 26-50 is not in play. Sources: PCG 2026/2 paras 14, 15, 29 to 43; ATO "Renting out part of a home".

### Example C: platform statement versus tax return

Guests paid $62,000 through the platform in 2026-27; $520 was refunded for cancellations through the platform; the platform withheld $9,150 of fees and commission; the host received $52,330. The platform's SERR report shows gross $61,480 and fees $9,150. Correct return: income $61,480; deduction $9,150 for platform fees (plus other direct costs). If the host declared only $52,330, income is understated by $9,150 and will not match the ATO's data. Cleaning fees charged to guests are part of income; the cost of cleaning is a separate deduction. Sources: ATO "Income and deductions for renting out your home"; BIG fields SETPP53, SETPP56.

### Example D: below-market rent to a relative (PCG 2026/2 paras 44 to 46)

Owner rents a property to a brother at $400 a week for 52 weeks = $20,800 (market rate would be higher). Ownership costs that would otherwise be deductible: $34,000. The whole $20,800 is assessable. Deductions are apportioned to exclude the private element; where they would exceed rent received the ATO accepts limiting them to rent received: deduction $20,800, net nil, no loss. If instead $20,800 were the market rent, evidenced when the rent was set (valuation or comparable listings), no apportionment is needed: deductions $34,000, loss $13,200 (before any 2027-28 quarantine). Not a short-stay case, but the same principle sits behind discounted stays by friends. Sources: TR 2026/1 paras 65 to 68; PCG 2026/2 paras 44 to 49.

### Example E: GST turnover and registration (residential rent excluded)

Host A: rent from short-stay houses $180,000 (residential, input taxed) and consulting income $60,000. Current GST turnover = $60,000 (input taxed rent excluded, s 188-15(1)(a)). Below the threshold: not required to register. Host A also takes $20,000 a year of management fees from other owners (taxable supplies): turnover $80,000, which is $5,000 over the threshold stated on the ATO's Registering for GST page: Host A must register within 21 days of reaching it (the ATO says to check monthly). Once registered, the rent stays input taxed and credits on rental costs stay denied (s 11-15(2)(a)); credits are available only on costs relating to the consulting and management activity. Sources: s 23-5, s 188-15, ATO Registering for GST page.

### Example F: Division 87 in a registered CRP operator, nightly rate $330 GST inclusive ($300 plus $30)

Stay of 40 continuous nights in premises **not** predominantly for long-term accommodation.
- Nights 1 to 27 at full price: 27 x $330 = $8,910; GST 27 x $30 = $810.
- Nights 28 to 40 (13 nights): value = 50% of price if Division 87 did not apply = 0.5 x (13 x $330 = $4,290) = $2,145; GST = 10% x $2,145 = $214.50, that is $16.50 a night; charged $316.50 a night; $4,114.50 for the 13 nights.
- Total GST $1,024.50; total charged $13,024.50.
If the premises were predominantly long-term (70% or more of bookings 28 nights or longer): value = 50% x $13,200 = $6,600; GST $660. If the operator has chosen not to apply Division 87 (s 87-25): the whole 40-night stay is input taxed, GST nil (the operator's pricing is its own choice) and no credits on its costs. A 20-night stay is fully taxable: GST 20 x $30 = $600. The ATO's own worked examples use the same half-of-the-GST-inclusive-price method. Sources: ss 87-5, 87-10, 87-20, 87-25; GSTR 2012/7; ATO CRP page.

### Example G: counting to 28

Check-in 1 March 2027, check-out 28 March 2027: the arrival day counts, the departure day is disregarded, so 27 days, not long-term accommodation. Check-out 29 March: 28 days, long-term accommodation. Source: s 87-20(2).

### Example H: 2027-28 loss quarantine preview (dwelling acquired after 12 May 2026, not a new dwelling)

Assessable income from the dwelling and deductions after apportionment: 2027-28 income $40,000, deductions $52,000; 2028-29 income $45,000, deductions $38,000; 2029-30 income $46,000, deductions $30,000.
- 2027-28: excess $12,000 is not deductible; deduct $40,000; net nil; carry forward $12,000.
- 2028-29: deductions plus carried amount = $38,000 + $12,000 = $50,000 against income $45,000; deduct $45,000; carry forward $5,000.
- 2029-30: deductions plus carried amount $30,000 + $5,000 = $35,000 against $46,000; all deductible; net rental income $11,000; nothing carried.
The carried amount can also reduce residential capital gains (s 26-155(1)(b) with s 102-5). Excludes grandfathered and new dwellings. Source: ITAA 1997 s 26-155 (Act 49 of 2026 Sch 2).

### Example I: main residence partly used for whole-home short stays (ATO method; `cgt` owns the arithmetic)

Owner buys a home (settled 1 Jul 2020), lives in it, and from 1 Jul 2022 lets the whole home short-stay while away, 320 nights in total until sale (settled 30 Jun 2026) at $1,000,000. Market value at 1 Jul 2022 was $850,000 (valuation on file). First income use is after 20 Aug 1996 and the home was fully exempt beforehand, so s 118-192 applies.
1. Gain from market value at first income use: $1,000,000 - $850,000 = $150,000 (ignoring other cost base items).
2. Floor area used for income: whole home, 100%.
3. Days from first income use to sale, inclusive: 1 Jul 2022 to 30 Jun 2026 = 4 x 365 + 1 leap day = 1,461.
4. Taxable part before any discount = $150,000 x 100% x 320 / 1,461 = **$32,854.21**.
5. The 50% discount for individuals on gains from CGT events before 1 Jul 2027 (s 115-100 as amended by Act 49 of 2026 Sch 1 item 28, https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2026A00049/asmade/2026-06-26/text/original/pdf) and any interaction with other losses belong to `cgt`.
Source: ATO "Using your home for rental or business" steps 1 to 6; s 118-190; s 118-192. Days counted inclusively as in the ATO example (2,192 days for 1 Jul 2019 to 30 Jun 2025).
