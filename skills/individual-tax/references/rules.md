# Calculation order and edge cases

The `individual_income_tax` tool applies these steps. Figures are named by key; values come from the rates files.

1. **Tax on taxable income.** Residents: `individual.resident_rates`. Part-year residents: the tax-free threshold becomes `individual.part_year_tax_free_threshold_flat` plus `individual.part_year_tax_free_threshold_prorated` times months resident over twelve (ITRA s20); the first taxed band then starts at that reduced threshold and the other band edges do not move. Foreign residents: `individual.foreign_resident_rates` (no tax-free threshold). Working holiday makers: `individual.whm_rates`.
2. **LITO** (`individual.lito`), residents only (including part-year), capped at gross tax. It does not reduce the Medicare levy, the surcharge or study loan repayments.
3. **Medicare levy** at `medicare.levy_rate` on taxable income, then:
   - s7: nil at or below `medicare.low_income_single_lower`; up to the phase-in limit (`medicare.low_income_single_upper`) the levy is capped at `medicare.low_income_shade_in_rate` of the excess.
   - s8 (spouse on 30 June, or sole parent with dependent children): family income threshold = `medicare.low_income_family_lower` plus `medicare.low_income_per_child_lower_add` per child. Nil if family taxable income does not exceed it; otherwise reduction = levy rate x threshold less (shade-in rate less levy rate) x (family income less threshold). If the spouse would also pay levy, the reduction is scaled by own taxable income over family income (s8(3)). Excess reduction passing to the spouse (s8(4)) is not modelled.
   - s9: full-exemption days remove the levy pro rata; half-exemption days remove half. Foreign residents are prescribed persons all year (no levy). Part-year residents' non-resident days are full-exemption days.
   - Years whose low-income thresholds are not yet set (2026-27 as at 29 Sep 2026): the tool only proceeds when taxable income, and family income where relevant, exceed the current-law limits (the `medicare.low_income_current_law_*` keys) by a further phase-in ratio margin. Otherwise it refuses with AU-GEN-003 (the thresholds have no value, so no draft is possible).
4. **Medicare levy surcharge** (residents; not for full-exemption days). Income for MLS = taxable income + reportable fringe benefits + net investment losses + reportable super contributions + exempt foreign employment income. Tier from `medicare.mls_single_tiers`, or `medicare.mls_family_tiers` (raised by `medicare.mls_family_per_child_after_first` for each child after the first) on combined family income. Charged on taxable income plus reportable fringe benefits, pro rata for days without cover. Married people must also have own MLS income above the singles lower Medicare threshold (s8D(3)(c)); sole parents need own income above the family tier 1 threshold (s8C(3)).
5. **Study loan repayment**: `help.repayment_schedule` applied to repayment income (same components as MLS income). From 2025-26 it is marginal up to the top band, where the rate applies to all repayment income. Capped at the outstanding balance if given.
6. **Total liability** = income tax after offsets + Medicare levy + surcharge + study loan repayment. Effective rate = total over taxable income. The marginal rate reported covers income tax (after LITO) and the Medicare levy on the next dollar; surcharge and study loan thresholds are cliffs and are excluded.

Completeness is returned before the amounts as `limitations`, `total_complete`, `total_status` and `completeness_scope`. Each limitation gives an id, kind (exclusion or assumption), message and affected fields. An unmodelled spouse levy excess or missing net labour income for the working Australians tax offset makes the component total incomplete. Unstated hospital cover with a possible surcharge (including unavailable surcharge tiers), assumed spouse MLS income, assumed sole-parent family tax benefit, exemption days estimated from resident months, or an uncapped positive study-loan repayment with no balance makes it conditional. No contingent surcharge at the applicable income threshold does not change completeness. Any `upstream_limitations` passed in (for example from `assemble_taxable_income`) are added with their own kind, so an upstream assumption makes the total at least conditional and an upstream exclusion makes it incomplete. Ordinary rate rules and rounding are not missing-input limitations. Present these limitations before the result and retain them on dependent totals; never describe a false `total_complete` as complete. A clean result is complete only within the modelled component scope, with other offsets and final settlement still subject to reconciliation.

Boundary convention: a threshold figure belongs to the band below it (ATO tables read "up to and including"), so the next band starts one dollar above it.
