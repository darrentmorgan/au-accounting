# Rental property (Australia, individual owners)

Works out the income tax result for a residential rental property held by an individual who is not carrying on a business, through three tools: `rental_property_result`, `capital_works_deduction` and, for rent from property outside Australia only, `foreign_rental_net`. Never do the arithmetic yourself and never quote a rate, date or threshold from memory; every figure comes from the tool's `figures_used`.

## Scope

In scope (individual, Australian residential property, passive investment):
- Rent and platform income, and which expenses are deductible now, apportioned, denied or capital.
- Interest (purpose test), borrowing expenses spread over the loan period (ITAA 1997 s 25-25), repairs v initial repairs and improvements (s 25-10, TR 97/23).
- Depreciating assets (Div 40) including the denial for second-hand assets in residential rentals (s 40-27), and capital works (Div 43): general rate, the higher traveller accommodation rate, construction start date rules.
- Travel to a residential rental (s 26-31, denied) and holding costs of vacant land (s 26-102, denied).
- Apportionment for private use, part-year use, a room or area let, and rent below market to family or friends (TR 2026/1, PCG 2026/2).
- Holiday homes also rented: s 26-50, TR 2026/1, PCG 2026/3 zones.
- Short-stay (Airbnb-style) letting by an individual owner: the rental result comes from this skill. Night-by-night apportionment of private and blocked-out use, the GST screen and the platform reporting regime are in the short-stay-accommodation skill; take its `rental_property_result_handoff` as the input to `rental_property_result`.
- The residential dwelling loss quarantine from the 2027-28 income year (ss 26-155, 26-160), for awareness and previews.

Out of scope (the tool refuses; see Escalation): property development, renovation for resale and subdivision (revenue v capital), commercial property, property owned by a company, trust, partnership or SMSF, a rental business or large portfolio, mixed business use. Property overseas is not worked through `rental_property_result`; see the overseas path in the Procedure. GST on short-stay accommodation (commercial residential premises v residential premises) and the platform reporting regime are for the short-stay-accommodation skill, with BAS mechanics in gst-bas. CGT on sale, main residence exemption and cost base are for the cgt skill. Taxable income and tax payable are for individual-tax.

## Required inputs

Ask for anything missing that changes the answer. Do not assume silently.

1. **Income year** (`2025-26` or `2026-27`). "This year" is ambiguous; confirm it.
2. Owner: individual? Ownership share by legal title (joint tenants equal; tenants in common by deed share).
3. Property: residential dwelling? Long-term or short-stay? Overseas? Vacant land or a dwelling built or renovated while held?
4. Rent and platform income received, before agent and platform fees, and excluding GST.
5. Expenses for the period, by kind: agent, platform, advertising, guest cleaning; interest (and any part of the loan used for private purposes, redraws, splits); rates, water, land tax, insurance, body corporate, repairs, gardening; borrowing costs with loan start date and term; any travel; initial repairs, improvements, purchase and sale costs.
6. Use pattern: days let, days genuinely available for rent on commercial terms, private-use days, whether only a room or part of the home is let (floor areas: tenant's area, shared areas, total), whether rent is below market to relatives, dates of any clear change in use, days owned.
7. Holiday homes: do the owner, family or friends use it for holidays at no or reduced rent? Are peak periods blocked out? How is it advertised and priced?
8. Assets: depreciating assets (cost, effective life, new or second-hand, dates) and construction (cost, start and completion dates, use type, units or bedrooms owned or leased).
9. For loss previews: date the ownership interest was last acquired (contract date if bought under contract), whether the dwelling was newly built, other residential dwellings owned.

## Judgement rules

- **Purpose test.** Interest is deductible by what the borrowed money was used for, not what secures it (TR 2000/2). A redraw used privately is a private borrowing even on a rental loan. Enter the private share as `interest_non_rental_share`. Offset accounts reduce interest and are not redraws.
- **Repairs v improvements.** Repairs fix wear and tear or damage that happened while renting; they are deductible now. Repairs of defects present when bought (initial repairs), improvements and replacing an entirety are capital: capital works over the Div 43 period, or Div 40 for depreciating assets (TR 97/23). Enter them in `initial_repairs` or `capital_improvements`, and as `capital_works` items or assets when a schedule exists.
- **Travel.** Not deductible for an individual owner of a residential rental (s 26-31), including trips to inspect or collect rent. A rental business would change this and is out of scope.
- **Vacant land.** Holding costs are denied from 1 Jul 2019 (s 26-102; TR 2023/3). A dwelling built or substantially renovated while held keeps the land vacant until it can be lawfully occupied and is let or available for rent. Exceptions (business use, certain entities) are out of scope.
- **Second-hand assets.** Denied in residential rentals unless acquired before the 2017 cut-off and installed before 1 Jul 2017 (dates come from `figures_used`). New assets you buy are fine. Quantity surveyor schedules often include denied items.
- **Effective lives.** Use the Commissioner's current determination (Income Tax (Effective Life of Depreciating Assets) Determination 2025) or a recorded own estimate. TR 2022/1 has been withdrawn; do not cite it.
- **Apportionment.** Time factor: days let plus days genuinely available on commercial terms, over days in the period. Area factor: tenant's own area plus half the shared area, over the total area. Multiply them when both apply. A room in the home has no available days when unoccupied. Agent, platform, advertising and guest cleaning costs are not apportioned. Blocking peak periods, advertising only to friends or above-market pricing means days are not available on commercial terms (PCG 2026/2 paras 14-17).
- **Family rent.** Rent below market is still assessable. Deductions are apportioned and, under PCG 2026/2, limited to the rent received so there is no loss. Market rent to relatives needs evidence made when the rent was set.
- **Holiday homes (s 26-50).** If not used or held mainly to produce rent, all ownership and use costs are denied, with no apportionment; advertising, agent fees and cleaning after guests remain deductible. The test is objective and qualitative, not a bright-line count of days (TR 2026/1 paras 38-43). A clear, sustained change in main use during the year allows a part-year exception (s 26-50(4)); seasonal patterns do not. The ATO will not devote compliance resources to s 26-50 for expenses before 1 Jul 2026 (compliance approach only). For a genuine rental with limited private use, apportion for that use.
- **Co-owners.** Income and deductions follow legal title. Run the tool with the taxpayer's `ownership_percent` and whole-property amounts.
- **Short-stay owners.** Income is assessable gross; platform fees are deducted separately. Platforms report short-term accommodation income to the ATO, so amounts must reconcile. Letting one or two properties is usually passive; hotel-like services, many properties or a management business point to a business (escalate). Whether GST applies is a separate question: use short-stay-accommodation (residential premises v commercial residential premises, platform reporting) and gst-bas for BAS mechanics.
- **Negative gearing (2026 law).** Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026 commenced on 27 Jun 2026 but the loss quarantine applies from the 2027-28 income year. In 2025-26 and 2026-27 rental losses are deductible against other income as before; never say they are quarantined. From 2027-28, excess deductions over income from residential dwellings are not deductible against other income and carry forward against future residential rental income (and can be applied against residential capital gains). Dwellings whose ownership interest was last acquired before 7.30 pm ACT time on the cut-off day in `figures_used` (contract date counts) are not caught, nor are new residential dwellings or ministerially determined purposes. The instrument defining a new residential dwelling has not been made, so say the exception cannot be assessed yet (AU-RENT-002) rather than guessing. Hotels, motels, caravans, boats and student accommodation are not residential dwellings. Extensions apply on death of a spouse or co-owner and relationship breakdown.
- **Owner abroad (lead with the escalation).** An individual who lets Australian property and says they have moved overseas or are a foreign resident, where residency rests only on that statement (no dates, days in Australia, ties or intention given) and who asks what tax they pay on the rent, about the tax-free threshold, or about a sale: open with AU-RES-004 verbatim (AU-RES-001 instead where a treaty country such as Singapore is in play; both messages are in `data/refusals/residency.yaml`), before any headline. Give no tax payable, taxable income, total, rate application, Medicare or CGT amount, no worked illustration with made-up figures, and call no calculator on the rent or the sale. Orientation in words only: a foreign resident is generally taxed on Australian-source rent at foreign resident rates with no tax-free threshold and no Medicare levy, a foreign resident at the sale contract date generally cannot use the main residence exemption, and withholding can apply on a sale. Hand the residency question to `residency-cross-border` and the review to a registered tax agent with cross-border experience. Do not call `rental_property_result` for them until residency is settled. Where residency is a settled input to a stated computation, calculate the rental result as usual and pass it to individual-tax.
- **Main residence rented out.** Renting part or all of a home can affect the main residence exemption; refer to the cgt skill.
- **CGT interplay.** Capital works claimed reduce the cost base; non-deductible holding costs and initial repairs may add to it (cgt skill).

- **Rentals owned by a trust, company, partnership or SMSF.** Say this skill handles individual owners only, raise AU-RENT-001, and stop. No description of how a loss is used, carried forward or allocated, even where general principles are well known: the loss stays with the entity and its later use goes to a registered tax agent (AU-TRUST-004 for a trust).
