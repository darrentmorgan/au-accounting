# Australia-Indonesia cross-border (treaty working paper)

Allocates taxing rights under the Australia-Indonesia agreement, counts the treaty days, converts Indonesian tax into a foreign income tax offset and works out Australian withholding for an Indonesian payee, through the tools named below. Never do arithmetic or day counts yourself and never quote a treaty ceiling, day limit or rate from memory: every figure comes from a tool's `figures_used`. If the au-tax tools are not available in this session, say so, quote AU-GEN-001 with its message verbatim and stop.

Treaty allocation is not exemption. Australia still taxes its residents on worldwide income (ITAA 1997 s 6-5(2)); the agreement's saving clause (MLI Art 11) keeps that, and the relief is the Art 24 credit, applied here as the foreign income tax offset (ITAA 1997 Div 770).

## Scope and boundary

This skill owns the Indonesia treaty article analysis. The `residency-cross-border` skill keeps the general residency tests, the generic foreign income tax offset and the scope check for other cross-border structures; both skills use the existing `indonesia_dta_check` and `foreign_income_tax_offset` tools, and this skill never duplicates them.

In scope (individuals, small companies and sole traders; income years 2025-26, 2026-27 and 2027-28):
- Arts 4(3), 10, 11, 12, 14 and 15 through `indonesia_dta_check`, with the day counts from `indonesia_presence_window`.
- Art 5(2)(j) service permanent establishment screen (`indonesia_service_pe_screen`).
- Allocation for real property income (Art 6), business profits (Art 7), gains (Art 13), directors' fees (Art 16), entertainers (Art 17), pensions (Art 18), other income (Art 22) through `indonesia_treaty_allocation`.
- Elimination of double tax (Art 24) as an offset: `indonesia_treaty_fito`, which applies treaty ceilings and then runs `foreign_income_tax_offset`.
- Inbound Australian withholding on dividends, interest and royalties to an Indonesian resident (`au_withholding_indonesian_payee`).
- Rupiah to AUD (`idr_to_aud`) from ATO published rates, refusing where a rate is unpublished or the basis is not allowed.

Out of scope (escalate, do not answer; `indonesia_scope_check` returns the code and message):
- Indonesian domestic tax: whether someone is an Indonesian domestic resident, rates and withholding under Indonesian law, VAT, final tax on rent, land and building taxes, transfer duties, permits, penalties, refunds, and whether Indonesian tax was correctly imposed (AU-IDN-002). Nothing about Indonesian domestic law is verified in this plugin.
- Permanent establishment or fixed base determinations, dependent agents, building sites and installations (AU-IDN-001).
- An Indonesian company (PT or PT PMA), a trust or partnership with Indonesian income, related-party charges between Australian and Indonesian entities (AU-IDN-003). An Australian resident who controls a PT is also AU-RES-002 (controlled foreign company rules; Indonesia is not a listed country).
- Land-rich shares, or a villa or land held through an entity (AU-IDN-004).
- Software or licence payments where royalty status is unclear, conduit payees, principal purpose test concerns (AU-IDN-005).
- Dual-resident companies (MLI Art 4; AU-RES-001), foreign pensions and super (AU-RES-003), any other treaty (AU-RES-001), transfer pricing.
- The GST or tax treatment of Bali short-stay operations, the tax on the capital gain itself (cgt skill), the rental deductions and the net foreign rent (`foreign_rental_net`, rental-property skill), and lodging or paying (AU-GEN-002).

## Required inputs

Ask for what is missing. Do not assume silently.
1. **Income year** (`2025-26`, `2026-27` or `2027-28`). For 2027-28 the treaty ceilings and Australian withholding rates are loaded and verified, but the rupiah rates and the foreign assets reporting threshold are not published: those steps refuse AU-GEN-003 (quote it; a rate the user supplies with its source still works with `idr_to_aud`), as does any step resting on another domain's unpublished 2027-28 figure. Never assume the current year.
2. **Direction**: an Australian resident with Indonesian income (most matters), or an Indonesian resident receiving Australian payments or working in Australia.
3. **What the income is**: villa rent, consulting or business profits, employment (who the employer is and where it is resident), directors' or commissioners' fees, dividends (who holds the shares, personally or through a company), interest, royalties, pension, sale of property or shares.
4. **Days**: each visit to Indonesia as first and last day (all presence counts), across at least the prior 12 months, not only the income year.
5. For company staff: who worked in Indonesia, on which dates, on which project.
6. **Amounts and tax paid**: gross amounts before withholding, Indonesian tax paid on each item and when (slips or assessment), in rupiah or AUD; taxable income, deductions related to the Indonesian income, family details for Medicare, private hospital cover.
7. For a sale: date, rupiah proceeds, each cost base element with its own date, holding period, and the cgt skill's net capital gain.

## Procedure

1. Confirm the income year, direction and question. Say which figures are for which year.
2. Scope check. If any out-of-scope situation above appears in the facts, call `indonesia_scope_check` with the matching `situations`, quote the returned code and message verbatim and stop that part. Continue with the parts that are in scope. Do not offer a view on Indonesian domestic tax even informally.
3. Allocate the income. For each income type call `indonesia_treaty_allocation` with `income_type`, `residence_state`, `source_state` and the facts it needs (`pe_or_fixed_base_in_source_state`, `land_rich`, `enterprise_is_company`). For employment, independent services, dividends, interest and royalties it points to `indonesia_dta_check` (or `au_withholding_indonesian_payee` for the Australian side); call that tool.
4. Days. For Art 14 (independent services) or Art 15 (employment):
   - Whenever Art 15 is relevant, including when the answer stops at an escalation (residency not settled, AU-IDN-002, no trip dates yet), state the rule in full: an Australian resident employed by an Australian employer who works in Indonesia is taxable in Indonesia unless all four Art 15(2) conditions hold (presence not over the day limit in any 12-month period; employer not resident in Indonesia; remuneration not borne by a permanent establishment or fixed base there; remuneration taxed in Australia). Give the limit as a number: `indonesia_presence_window` returns it, and without trip data read it with `get_figure` (`residency.indonesia_dta_days_threshold`). Never write only "a day limit". Say whether the stated stay could meet the first condition (a stay of about two years, working while there, cannot). Indonesian domestic tax stays out of scope (AU-IDN-002).
   - Call `indonesia_presence_window` with every trip. Report the maximum days in any rolling 12 months, its period, the per-income-year subtotals and the limit from `figures_used`.
   - Then call `indonesia_dta_check` with `article` `employment` or `independent_services` and `days_in_other_state_12m` set to the tool's handoff value, plus the other facts (`fixed_base_available`, `employer_resident_in_work_state`, `remuneration_borne_by_pe_or_fixed_base`, `remuneration_taxed_in_residence_state`, `residence_state`). A null result means a fact is missing: ask for it.
   - Tie-breaker questions (`residence_tie_breaker`) only arise if the person is a resident of both countries under each country's own law. Indonesian domestic residence is not verified here, so it is always unknown unless an Indonesian adviser has said otherwise: raise AU-IDN-002 every time.
   - A tie-breaker result is an indication, never a determination. Headline it as where the Art 4(3) steps point on the facts supplied ("the steps point towards Australia, as an indication only"). Never write "you are resident of Australia only", "resident of Indonesia only" or "under the treaty you are resident of X", and never open with a conclusion.
   - Walk the three steps in this agreement's order (permanent home available, habitual abode, closer personal and economic relations), say a home let to guests for most of the year may not count as "available" (OECD Commentary on Art 4, an interpretive aid the ATO treats as relevant under TR 2001/13, not treaty text), and list every fact that could change the indication (the tool's `missing_facts`: Indonesian domestic residence, availability of each home, comparative stays for habitual abode, personal and economic relations).
   - In Refusals or escalations quote AU-RES-001 (a tie-breaker determination on facts that are incomplete) and AU-IDN-002 (Indonesian domestic residence) verbatim from the tool's `escalations`. Never write "none" for a tie-breaker answer. The determination goes to a registered tax agent, or the competent authority process, with an Indonesian adviser for the Indonesian-law question.
5. Service permanent establishment. For an Australian company or sole trader using employees or other personnel in Indonesia, call `indonesia_service_pe_screen` with each person's periods and `same_or_connected_project`. Report both counts. If the counts disagree the tool returns AU-IDN-001: quote the code and message verbatim, give both numbers, and state no conclusion. If both are over the limit, say a service permanent establishment is indicated, that Indonesia may tax the profits attributable to it (Art 7), and that attribution is not computed.
6. Rupiah. Call `idr_to_aud` for each rupiah amount: regular income and expenses use the annual or monthly ATO average (`nature` `income_or_deduction_spread_over_year`); a sale or purchase needs the rate at the event date (`supplied_rate` with source, or `actual_30_jun` only on 30 June); Indonesian tax is translated when paid. The ATO does publish rupiah rates; never say it does not. If the tool returns AU-GEN-003 the ATO figure is not yet published: quote it, do not estimate, and offer a labelled supplied rate with its source instead. If it returns AU-IDN-006, quote it and ask for the event date rate and its source.
7. Villa rent. First test for complete facts: gross rent, each cost, the Indonesian tax paid, owner or family use and any loan all stated, and a computed result asked for. If the question is open (how to declare, credit for the Indonesian tax, can I claim depreciation or interest) or the facts are thin (a rounded figure already net of Indonesian tax, no gross and cost split), escalate instead: open with AU-RENT-005 verbatim, orientation in words only, no offset amount, no net Australian result, no confirmed or leaned deduction, no calculator, hand-off to a registered tax agent with cross-border experience and an Indonesian adviser. On complete facts the path for rent from a Bali villa (or any Indonesian property) is fixed; do not send it to `rental_property_result` (it refuses AU-RENT-005 for property outside Australia, and that refusal must never end the answer).
   - Convert the gross rent, each cost and the Indonesian tax with `idr_to_aud` (step 6).
   - Call `foreign_rental_net` (rental-property skill) with those AUD amounts: `gross_rent_aud`, `expenses` (mark interest and other debt deductions `debt_deduction: true`) and `foreign_tax_paid_aud`. It returns the net foreign rent, the components for `assemble_taxable_income` (`foreign_income` gross plus `other_deduction` lines) and the `indonesia_treaty_fito_inputs` (the `rent_real_property` item and `related_deductions_aud`).
   - Quote AU-RENT-005 from its `escalations` as the part not checked (deductibility of the stated costs for foreign property, depreciation, capital works, interest apportionment, private use), then go on to step 8. It is a scope note on that part, not a stop for the paper.
8. Foreign income tax offset. For a resident individual with Indonesian tax paid, call `indonesia_treaty_fito`:
   - one item per income stream (`kind`, `gross_aud`, `foreign_tax_paid_aud`), taking the villa rent item and `related_deductions_aud` from `foreign_rental_net`; for a capital gain pass the gain before discount as `gross_aud` and the cgt skill's net capital gain for that asset as `assessable_aud`;
   - `taxable_income` (including the assessable Indonesian income), `related_deductions_aud` (deductions related to the Indonesian income, excluding debt deductions), family details, `overseas_assets_max_value_aud` if the return's overseas assets question is asked.
   Whenever a FITO is claimed for Indonesian tax, say in the Result: (i) the relief is the treaty credit (Art 24) applied as the foreign income tax offset under ITAA 1997 Div 770 (creditable amount s 770-10 and s 770-15, limit s 770-75); (ii) the Indonesian tax counts only if correctly imposed under Indonesian law and in accordance with the treaty, which this plugin cannot verify (AU-IDN-002), so a user's "treat it as correct" is an assumption to list, not a finding; (iii) evidence to keep and ask for: the Indonesian tax assessment or return and the payment receipts (or withholding slips), showing the amount, date paid and income taxed, with the rupiah rate source.
   Report per item the treaty ceiling, the creditable tax, the excess over the ceiling (to be reclaimed from Indonesia, not offset) and the offset result. Do not also call `foreign_income_tax_offset` for the same amounts. `exit_code` 3 with AU-RES-005: quote it and stop. `exit_code` 4: quote AU-GEN-001 or AU-GEN-003 and do not guess (for example the 2026-27 overseas assets threshold is unpublished).
9. Inbound withholding. For Australian payments to an Indonesian resident call `au_withholding_indonesian_payee` (`payment_kind`, amounts, `payee_resident_of_indonesia`, `beneficially_entitled`). A payment connected with the payee's Australian permanent establishment returns AU-IDN-001. An Indonesian resident selling Australian real property or land-rich interests: state that Australia may tax the gain (Art 13(1), 13(4)), that foreign resident capital gains withholding sits with the cgt skill, and escalate land-rich or indirect interests (AU-IDN-004). An Indonesian resident employee working in Australia is taxed on the foreign resident scale through individual-tax, and Art 15 still allocates the remuneration.
10. Read every envelope: `exit_code` 0 present; 2 fix the input; 3 quote the refusal verbatim and stop that part; 4 quote AU-GEN-001 or AU-GEN-003 (AU-GEN-001 can be rerun as a marked draft; AU-GEN-003 cannot).
11. Hand results on: taxable foreign income and `tax_payable_after_fito` to individual-tax (gross foreign income in the assembly, offset reported beside the tax); the tax-side of a villa sale to cgt.

## Judgement rules

Instrument and status
- The agreement is [1992] ATS 40, in force since December 1992 and given force through ITAA 1953 s 5 (the table row for the Indonesian agreement); its text is not scheduled to the Act. It prevails over inconsistent assessment law (ITAA 1953 s 4(2)) except Part IVA and Subdiv 195-C (s 4(3)), so the general anti-avoidance rule can override a treaty outcome. There is no protocol; the MLI modifies it for every income year from 2021-22, so both supported years use the modified text.
- MLI provisions that apply: preamble (Art 6), dual-resident entities (Art 4), principal purpose test (Art 7), land-rich shares (Art 9), saving clause (Art 11), anti-fragmentation of specific-activity exemptions (Art 13), splitting up of building-site contracts (Art 14) and closely related (Art 15). Australia did not adopt the MLI commissionaire rule, so agent permanent establishments stay under the agreement's own Art 5(4) and (5).
- This agreement has no non-discrimination article. Do not import one (individual-tax AU-IND-004 covers the working holiday maker escalation).

Days and residence
- The limits in Arts 14(1)(b) and 15(2)(a) are the value of `residency.indonesia_dta_days_threshold`, measured in any period of 12 months on a rolling basis, not the income year, and not the domestic 183-day residency test. Art 5(2)(j) uses the separate key `au_indonesia.dta_pe_services_days`; Art 5(2)(h) and (i) use `au_indonesia.dta_pe_resource_installation_days` and `au_indonesia.dta_pe_building_site_days`.
- Presence counts every day physically present, including part days, weekends, holidays and arrival and departure days, not only working days. Whenever you count days for Art 14 or 15, say in the answer that the treaty does not define a "day" of presence, that counting all days physically present follows the OECD Commentary on Art 15 as an interpretive aid the ATO treats as relevant (TR 2001/13), and so the count is a reading and not settled. Still give the count against the limit (read the limit with `get_figure`, key `residency.indonesia_dta_days_threshold`) and the result, and if only working days are counted by the user, show why the holiday days are added. Exactly at the limit is "not exceeding" for Art 15 and not "exceeding" for Art 14.
- Art 15(2) exemption needs all four conditions: presence not over the limit; employer not a resident of the state where the work is done; remuneration not borne by a permanent establishment or fixed base there; remuneration taxed in the residence state. If any fails, Indonesia may tax the remuneration for the Indonesian work, not only the days above the limit. An Indonesian company as employer fails from the first day. Art 15 is subject to Arts 16, 17, 18, 19 and 20 (Art 17, entertainers, overrides Arts 14 and 15).
- Art 14(1)(a): a fixed base regularly available in Indonesia (a desk, co-working membership or home office used for the activity) lets Indonesia tax income attributable to it with no day count. Whether a Bali space is a fixed base is a fact question: escalate (AU-IDN-001). Art 14 covers individuals; a company, partnership or trustee is Art 7 and Art 5. Classifying software, IT or marketing work as professional services (Art 14(2) is inclusive) is a judgement: say so.
- Dual-resident company (for example an Australian company managed from Bali): do not say which country taxes its worldwide profits, do not assume the place of incorporation decides, and do not say the place of effective management decides. Under Art 4 of the MLI as it modifies this agreement, the old place-of-effective-management rule for non-individuals is replaced: the competent authorities try to settle the residence by mutual agreement, having regard to place of effective management, place of incorporation and other relevant factors, and without that agreement the company is not entitled to relief or exemption under the agreement. The ATO route for a dual-resident company is a request for a competent authority determination under MLI Art 4(1), lodged through the ATO MAP Program Management Unit (Indonesia is on the ATO's list of treaties modified by Art 4(1); ATO page "Mutual agreement procedure"). Do not tell a company to lodge an early engagement request for a certificate of residency: the ATO offers that early engagement only to individuals with conflicting residency facts (ATO page "Certificate of residency and overseas tax relief form"). Escalate AU-RES-001 and recommend a registered tax agent or international tax adviser.
- Directors' fees (Art 16) are taxable where the company is resident with no days test.
- Art 4(3) tie-breaker order for individuals is: permanent home, habitual abode, closer personal and economic relations. There is no nationality step and no mutual agreement step, and the order differs from the OECD Model. Whichever step decides on the facts given, the output is an indication only (see Procedure step 4), because each step is a question of fact and Indonesian domestic residence is unverified. A home let out to guests for most of the year may not be "available". Companies use MLI Art 4 (competent authority agreement); a dual-resident company is escalated.

Permanent establishment
- Art 5(2)(j) needs no fixed place of business: services (including consultancy) furnished through employees or other personnel for the same or a connected project over the limit in any 12 months. The agreement does not say how days are counted. Enterprise days (each day once) and person days can point opposite ways; present both and escalate. Art 5(3) exceptions are tightened by MLI Art 13; a subsidiary is not itself a permanent establishment of its parent (Art 5(6)).
- Art 7: profits of an enterprise are taxable in its state unless it has a permanent establishment in the other, then only those attributable to the establishment plus same-or-similar sales and activities. Art 9 associated enterprises and transfer pricing are out of scope (AU-IDN-003).

Property
- Art 6: rent from real property (letting, direct use, any use) is taxable where the property is, with no permanent establishment needed and no ceiling. An Australian resident is taxed here as well, with the offset. Interest on a villa loan is a debt deduction: it is not disregarded in the offset limit and is claimed as a normal deduction, and the ATO instructions for 2025-26 exclude foreign rental debt deductions from the foreign rent worksheet. Re-read the return labels each year.
- Art 13: real property gains (13(1)) and gains on land-rich entities (13(4), lookback in `au_indonesia.mli_land_rich_lookback_days`, extended to partnership and trust interests by MLI Art 9) may be taxed where the property is. Art 13(5) leaves gains on other property (shares in a company that is not land-rich) to each country's domestic law: there is no residence-only rule and no ceiling. Whether ITAA 1953 s 3A(4) is switched off by the MLI for Art 13(4) is unresolved: escalate.
- A villa sold in rupiah can be a capital loss in AUD because each cost base element is translated at its own date (ITAA 1997 s 960-50(6) item 5). An Indonesian tax on a gain earns an offset only on the part that is in the net capital gain.

Ceilings and the offset
- Arts 10 to 12 and 18 give ceilings, not rates. The readings are `residency.indonesia_dta_wht_dividends`, `residency.indonesia_dta_wht_interest`, `residency.indonesia_dta_wht_royalties_equipment_know_how`, `residency.indonesia_dta_wht_royalties_other` and `au_indonesia.dta_wht_pensions_annuities`. The branch profits additional tax cap is `au_indonesia.dta_branch_profits_additional_tax_cap`. Royalties for equipment or know-how take the lower tier; copyright, patents, trademarks and film take the higher. Natural resource payments are Art 6 and uncapped. Software licences: characterisation is unclear, escalate (AU-IDN-005).
- Only Indonesian tax correctly imposed under Indonesian law and in accordance with the agreement counts (ITAA 1997 s 770-15). Excess over a ceiling must be reclaimed from Indonesia. Penalties and interest are not foreign income tax. Foreign tax counts only on amounts included in assessable income (s 770-10), grossed up; a discounted gain apportions the foreign tax, and a nil net capital gain gives no offset.
- The limit is the greater of the default limit (`residency.fito_default_offset_limit`) and the extra tax caused by the foreign income (s 770-75, with Medicare levy and surcharge). The default-limit shortcut applies to the total creditable amount across all items, not each item alone. The offset is non-refundable, applied after other non-refundable offsets, with no carry-forward: unused foreign tax is lost.
- A dividend to an Australian company with the participation interest is normally non-assessable non-exempt income (ITAA 1997 s 768-5): the Indonesian tax on it earns no offset, whatever the underlying-tax rule in Art 24(2) (share in `au_indonesia.dta_underlying_tax_credit_min_voting_share`) suggests. Art 20 teacher visits (`au_indonesia.dta_teacher_visit_max_years`) and the mutual agreement case time (`au_indonesia.dta_map_case_presentation_years`) are reference facts, not tool outputs.
- ITAA 1936 s 23AG is now narrow (see residency-cross-border and `residency.s23ag_min_foreign_service_days`): ordinary employment in Bali does not become exempt. The 1992 explanatory memorandum's comfort about s 23AG and "comparable tax" countries is out of date; Indonesia is not a listed country for the controlled foreign company rules.

Withholding (Australian payer, Indonesian payee)
- Unfranked dividends are withheld at the lower of the domestic rate and the Art 10(2) ceiling (ITAA 1953 s 17A(1)); franked dividends carry none; interest is at the domestic rate, which equals the ceiling; royalties at the lower of the domestic rate and the Art 12(2) tier. The domestic rates are `au_indonesia.au_wht_unfranked_dividend_rate`, `au_indonesia.au_wht_interest_rate` and `au_indonesia.au_wht_royalty_rate`. The reduced rate needs an Indonesian resident payee who is beneficially entitled; a conduit gets the domestic rate and the principal purpose test can deny a ceiling.

Translation (ITAA 1997 s 960-50; ATO translation rules)
- A one-off capital event uses the rate at the event; each cost base element uses its own date. Regular rent and expenses may use an average that reasonably approximates the rates at the times. A year-end rate is not allowed for foreign income not received in Australia in the year derived. Indonesian tax is translated when paid. Where the ATO does not publish a rate, a rate from an Australian bank or another reliable external source is accepted, with the rate and source kept. Daily rates come from the Reserve Bank of Australia.

## Output

Frame everything as a working paper for review by a registered tax agent, not advice. End every answer with (CONVENTIONS section 8):

1. **Result** for the stated income year: for each income stream the article, which country may tax, the ceiling and the creditable tax, the excess to reclaim, the offset and limit, or the day counts and windows, or the withholding, or the AUD translation with the rate basis. Say plainly that Australia still taxes its resident and that Indonesian domestic tax was not verified. Where an offset is shown, cite Art 24 and ITAA 1997 Div 770, not only the limit section. Where Art 15 is relevant, state the day limit as a number and all four conditions, even if the paper stops at an escalation. A tie-breaker is reported as an indication with the missing facts, never as "resident of X only".
2. **Figures used**: key, value, status, source URL for each entry in `figures_used`.
3. **Assumptions**: the tools' `assumptions` plus any you made (for example that Indonesian tax stated as paid was correctly imposed, which the plugin cannot verify: AU-IDN-002; ask for the Indonesian assessment or payment receipts).
4. **Risk flags**: relevant entries from `data/risk_flags/au-indonesia-cross-border.yaml` (for example IDN-001 rolling window, IDN-002 Indonesian employer, IDN-003 service permanent establishment, IDN-005 ceiling treated as rate, IDN-006 discounted gains, IDN-008 debt deductions, IDN-012 translation basis) and RES-003 and RES-004 from `data/risk_flags/residency.yaml`, and `rental-foreign-property-stated-deductions` from `data/risk_flags/rental-property.yaml` for villa rent, or "none".

6. "Working paper only. Review by a registered tax agent (or BAS agent for BAS matters) before use." Copy this sentence verbatim as the last line of the answer; do not paraphrase it.
