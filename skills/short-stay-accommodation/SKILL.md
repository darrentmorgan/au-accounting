# Short-stay accommodation (Australia, individual owners)

Prepares working papers for the tax treatment of short-stay letting by an individual owner. It owns the short-stay facts and the GST screen, and hands the wider rental arithmetic to `rental-property` and BAS mechanics to `gst-bas`. Never do arithmetic yourself and never quote a rate, threshold, date or count from memory: every number comes from a tool's `figures_used` or a tool result.

| Tool | Use for |
|---|---|
| `short_stay_apportionment` | Night-by-night split of mixed-use costs, the holiday home gate, net rental result, and the handoff for `rental_property_result` |
| `gst_short_stay_classification` | Screen: residential premises (input taxed) or hotel-like features that must be escalated; handoff for `gst_registration_check` |
| `div87_stay_gst` | GST on one long stay in premises a registered agent has confirmed are commercial residential premises |
| `serr_report_check` | Would a platform report this booking; exemptions; platform report due dates |
| `serr_income_reconciliation` | Platform statements to gross income to declare, fees deduction, and any difference from what was declared |
| `rental_property_result` (rental-property) | Full rental result: depreciation, borrowing costs, capital works, negative gearing |
| `gst_registration_check` (gst-bas) | GST registration from turnover, with input taxed rent excluded |

## Scope

In scope: an individual (or co-owning individuals) letting a whole dwelling, a granny flat or a room to travellers, directly, through an agent or manager acting for the owner, or through a platform; passive investment, not a business.

- **GST**: residential premises v commercial residential premises (CRP), input taxed rent, no credits, GST turnover and registration, Division 87 arithmetic for a confirmed CRP, agent v principal supplier.
- **Income tax**: assessable gross income, three buckets of expenses, days available on commercial terms, holiday homes (s 26-50), own travel (s 26-31), second-hand assets (s 40-27), the 2027-28 loss quarantine as a preview.
- **Apportionment**: private use, family and friends, blocked-out nights, a room in the home.
- **Sharing Economy Reporting Regime (SERR)**: what platforms report and what it means for the host.

Out of scope, escalate (see Escalation): company, trust, partnership or SMSF owners; a business of letting or several properties run systematically; hotel-like operations; head-lease or principal operators; strata hotel pools beyond the owner's own position; foreign-resident hosts; property overseas. For sale of the property see `cgt`; tax on the resulting taxable income see `individual-tax`; PAYG instalment amounts see `payg-instalments-lodgment`; SA land tax and stamp duty see `state-taxes-sa` (short-stay points there are not settled, AU-SS-003).

## Required inputs

Ask for anything missing that changes the answer. Do not assume silently.

1. **Income year** (`2025-26`, `2026-27` or `2027-28`). "This year" is ambiguous; confirm it. 2027-28 is a leap year (1 Jul 2027 to 30 Jun 2028), so nights owned must add up to its number of days, which the tool reports if they do not. The 2027-28 overlay carries the Sharing Economy Reporting Regime dates and thresholds; a figure that is not published for 2027-28 returns AU-GEN-003.
2. **Owner**: individual? Co-owners and their title shares? Any company, trust, partnership or SMSF involved in owning, operating or financing?
3. **Property**: whole dwelling, granny flat, room in the owner's home, or an apartment in a managed complex? Is it the owner's main residence? In Australia? Days owned in the income year.
4. **Operation** (for the GST screen): who supplies the stay (owner, agent for the owner, an operator in its own right)? Daily cleaning, meals, reception, several premises booked centrally, marketed as a hotel, guest house or B&B?
5. **Nights**: every night owned, split into rented or paid for, unoccupied but available on commercial terms, owner use, family or friends free or reduced, blocked and unused, closed for repairs. The nights must add up to the days owned.
6. **Available on commercial terms**: advertised broadly, priced near comparables, requests monitored and not refused without reason? Which periods are blocked (peak periods)?
7. **Income and costs**: gross income before platform fees (including cleaning and host fees charged to guests), by platform; direct letting costs; ownership costs; area figures for part of a home.
8. **Other taxable turnover** (consulting, management fees charged to others) for GST registration.
9. **Platform statements** and what was declared, for reconciliation.

## Procedure

1. **Scope.** If an out-of-scope trigger applies, quote the code and message verbatim and stop that part (see Escalation). A company, trust, partnership or SMSF owner: give the scope statement, raise AU-RENT-001, call no calculator, and do not work out how a loss or income is allocated.
   **Foreign-resident or residency-unresolved host**: open with the residency escalation before anything else (see Lead-with rules), call no calculator and give no tax, CGT or levy figure.
2. **GST screen.** Call `gst_short_stay_classification` with `perspective`, `premises_kind`, the five operation indicators, `leased_together_with_whole_complex`, `annual_rent`, and `asked_must_register_because_of_platform_income` when the host asks about registering. Exit code 0 means residential premises, input taxed. Exit code 3 with AU-GST-002 means the tool will not decide: quote the code, set out the tests in `references/gst-tests.md`, and suggest a private ruling. A screen result is an indication from the facts given, not a ruling.
3. **Registration.** Take `handoff_gst_registration_check` from step 2 into `gst-bas` `gst_registration_check`, adding the host's other taxable turnover. Residential short-stay rent is excluded from GST turnover however large it is. If the host asks whether they must register because they earn through a platform, state the s 23-5 rule and the input taxed exclusion, note that the ATO Registering for GST page wording cannot be reconciled with the Act for residential rent, and quote AU-SS-001. Never say "you must register because you earn on a platform".
4. **Division 87.** Only when a registered agent has confirmed the premises are CRP: call `div87_stay_gst` with `crp_confirmed: true`, nights or check-in and check-out dates, `nightly_price_gst_inclusive`, the booking counts for the 12-month predominance test, and any s 87-25 election. Without confirmation it refuses AU-GST-002. Division 87 does nothing for a normal Airbnb house.
5. **Nights and costs.** Classify every night and every cost into the three buckets (`references/apportionment.md`). Call `short_stay_apportionment` with `days_owned`, the night fields, `part_of_home` and areas where a room or part of a home is let, `holiday_home_mainly_for_rent` whenever the owner, family or friends use or reserve the whole property (the tool refuses AU-RENT-003 if it is needed and missing), `gross_rent`, `direct_costs` and `ownership_costs` (lines). If the answer to the mainly-for-rent question is not clear from the facts, pass `unresolved` and let the tool refuse AU-RENT-003; never guess to reach a number. Nights closed for repairs refuse AU-SS-003.
6. **Full rental result.** Pass `rental_property_result_handoff` from step 5, unchanged, into `rental_property_result` with the expenses by category, depreciation, borrowing costs, capital works and `acquisition_date`. That tool owns negative gearing and the 2027-28 preview. Do not rebuild those numbers here.
7. **Platform data.** Call `serr_income_reconciliation` with every platform's statement and the income declared. Call `serr_report_check` for a booking, or a host, when asked what a platform reports. A property outside Australia refuses AU-SS-002.
8. **Read every envelope.** `exit_code` 0: present the result. `exit_code` 3 with `refusal`: quote the code and message verbatim, stop that part, say who should handle it. `exit_code` 4 with AU-GEN-001: a figure is not verified; offer a marked draft with `allow_draft`. AU-GEN-003: no published value, no draft possible. `exit_code` 2: fix the input and call again (nights must add up to days owned).
9. **If the au-tax tools are not available**, say so, quote AU-GEN-001 and its message verbatim, and stop. Do not compute by hand and do not read the rates files yourself.
10. Quote tool amounts in dollars and cents, exactly as returned. ATO examples truncate dollars, so a difference of a dollar against an ATO example is rounding.

## GST judgement rules

- **Length of stay never matters.** A house, unit, granny flat or room let to travellers is residential premises: the definition applies regardless of the term of occupation (GSTA 1999 s 195-1). The rent is input taxed (s 40-35(1)(a) and (2)(a)): no GST on the rent, no input tax credits on the related costs (s 11-15(2)(a)), and the rent is outside GST turnover (s 188-15). Council limits on short stays do not change this (GSTR 2012/5).
- **CRP is the exception, and a question of fact.** CRP is a hotel, motel, inn, hostel, boarding house or anything similar, plus caravan parks, school accommodation and some ships and marinas (s 195-1). Whether premises are CRP is impression and degree on the whole premises (GSTR 2012/6), weighing commercial operation, capacity for several unrelated guests, holding out to the public, accommodation as the main purpose, central management, the operator supplying in its own right rather than as agent, services provided, and guests rather than tenants. ATO conclusions to rely on: three bedrooms with communal dining, on-site owner, daily cleaning and breakfast is CRP (Example 2); two furnished rooms with linen only is not (Example 3); one strata apartment let through an on-site manager acting as agent is not (Example 12, in the current ruling, not only the draft). The draft update GSTR 2012/6DC is not final and the ATO says the Commissioner's view is unchanged: cite the current ruling.
- **Escalate the grey cases.** Any hotel-like indicator, an operator holding head leases or selling stays in its own name, or a whole-complex lease: AU-GST-002. State the tests; do not make the determination.
- **Owner and operator are different suppliers.** A strata apartment owner leasing to a hotel operator makes an input taxed lease, while the operator's supplies to guests are taxable CRP accommodation (GSTR 2012/6 paras 95-98; the South Steyne litigation, summarised in MBI Properties v FCT [2013] FCA 56). A manager acting as agent does not make the supply; its fee is a separate supply to the owner with no credit.
- **Platforms and fees.** The supplier of the stay is usually the host, not the platform. A platform GST line on its own fee does not create a credit: the host's credit rule is s 11-15(2)(a). The ATO's rule that an offshore platform assumes the user is unregistered unless given an ABN and declaration is in the ride-sourcing guidance; no accommodation equivalent was found, so do not state it for hosts.
- **Registration.** Required when the host carries on an enterprise and GST turnover reaches the registration threshold (s 23-5; keys `gst.registration_threshold`, `gst.registration_deadline_days`). Count only taxable supplies: consulting, management fees charged to other owners, taxable CRP sales, separately charged taxable extras. A registered owner still makes input taxed supplies when letting residential premises (s 9-30(2)).
- **Division 87** applies only to CRP, not residential premises. Long-term accommodation is provided for a continuous period of at least `gst.div87_long_term_days` days, counting the arrival day and disregarding the departure day (s 87-20). Predominantly long-term premises (bookings at or above `gst.div87_predominantly_long_term_share`, GSTR 2012/7 paras 53-56 counting bookings, actual last 12 months or projected next 12) value the supply at `gst.div87_value_share` of the price from day one (s 87-5). Otherwise only the days after the first `gst.div87_long_term_days` less one are reduced (s 87-10). The supplier may elect not to apply Division 87 for all its commercial accommodation, and cannot revoke the choice within 12 months (s 87-25).
- **Bonds** for residential premises carry no GST, and a retained bond is still assessable income.

## Income tax judgement rules

- **Assessable income.** Rent and platform income are assessable even if below market, whether received directly, through an agent or through a platform (TR 2026/1). Declare income before platform fees and commissions, including cleaning and host fees charged to guests, plus retained bonds, insurance payouts for lost rent and cancellation fees kept. Fees are a separate deduction. Rent is derived when the tenant pays, or when the agent or manager receives it. Platform payout timing at 30 June is not settled (AU-SS-003).
- **Investor, not business.** Renting, including on a platform, is not of itself a business; most owners of one or several properties are investors. Business indicators (TR 97/11 as applied in TR 2026/1 para 13) or hotel-like services take the owner outside TR 2026/1, PCG 2026/2 and PCG 2026/3: AU-RENT-001.
- **Three buckets** (PCG 2026/2). A: direct letting costs (platform commission, agent fees, advertising to find guests, cleaning and laundry after guest stays), deductible in full and still deductible where s 26-50 denies ownership costs. B: ownership and use costs (interest, borrowing costs, rates, water, land tax, body corporate, insurance, repairs, power and internet, decline in value, capital works), apportioned or, for a holiday home not mainly for rent, denied. C: denied, capital or private (own travel, second-hand assets, initial repairs and improvements, private use). Consumables restocked for guests are not addressed in ATO examples: treat as direct only if solely for guests, and say so.
- **Available on commercial terms.** A property is held to produce income when available for rent on commercial terms. For: broad advertising, comparable pricing, monitored requests. Against: advertised only to friends or at work, blocked for the owner or friends at below market during likely demand, unreasonable conditions, priced above comparables, requests not monitored or refused without reason. If the against factors dominate the ATO example denies every expense for the year, not an apportionment (`references/apportionment.md`).
- **Blocked-out nights** are not held to produce income. **A room in the owner's home has no held nights**: unoccupied nights are private even if the listing stays live. Nights paid for count as used even if nobody stayed.
- **Holiday homes (s 26-50).** A property is a holiday home if the owner, family or friends use it for holidays free or at a reduced rate, or the owner holds it for such use. Unless it is used or held mainly to produce rent at all times in the year, s 26-50 denies all ownership and use costs, with no apportionment. The test is objective and qualitative, not a day count (TR 2026/1 paras 37-43 and 104-114; PCG 2026/3 zones green, amber, red; do not invent day thresholds). Peak periods are location specific; blocked-out peaks with a property advertised most of the year point to not mainly rental. When the exception applies, ownership costs are still apportioned for private use. A part-year exception needs a definite and sustained change in the pattern of use, not a seasonal pattern (s 26-50(4)); the anti-avoidance rule in s 26-50(7) can override. Denied costs may form part of the CGT cost base (`cgt`). Green: pass `yes`. Red: pass `no`. Amber or unclear: pass `unresolved` and escalate AU-RENT-003.
- **2025-26 v 2026-27.** s 26-50 is unchanged law. The ATO will not devote compliance resources to s 26-50 for expenses incurred before `rental.holiday_home_compliance_start`. Never say the rule "does not apply" in 2025-26.
- **Family and friends.** Market-rate rent to family or friends needs evidence of the rate set at the time. Below-market rent is still assessable, deductions are apportioned or limited to the rent received (PCG 2026/2 paras 44-49). Free or reduced-rate stays also make it a holiday home. The tool counts those nights as private and flags that as a preparer's assumption.
- **Own travel (s 26-31).** An individual not in business cannot deduct their own trips to clean, hand over keys, inspect, collect rent or repair a short-stay dwelling, because residential premises take the GST meaning (LCR 2018/7). Paying a cleaner or manager is deductible.
- **Second-hand assets (s 40-27)** and **capital works**: a single house or unit is residential for Div 43 (the higher traveller rate needs the unit or bedroom minimum in `rental.capital_works_traveller_minimum_units`). Use `rental_property_result` and `capital_works_deduction`.
- **Co-owners** share income and expenses by legal title. A different equitable split is very limited.
- **Main residence let while away**: time-based apportionment on nights let, direct costs in full. Renting can reduce the main residence exemption: CGT is for `cgt` (partial exemption by floor area and days, market value reset under s 118-192 needs a valuation, never estimate one).
- **2027-28 loss quarantine.** The explanatory memorandum says a short-stay apartment can be a residential dwelling, so do not treat short-stay as outside the rule. A dwelling last acquired on or after `rental.negative_gearing_grandfather_date` (7.30 pm ACT time; contract date counts) is expected to be caught from `rental.negative_gearing_first_income_year`, unless an exception applies. Whether a hotel-managed serviced apartment is inside the rule is not settled (AU-SS-003). The definition of a new residential dwelling awaits an instrument (AU-RENT-002). In 2025-26 and 2026-27 losses are deductible as before.
- **Instalments and records.** Platform payouts arrive with no tax withheld; consider voluntary PAYG instalments (`payg_instalments.entry_instalment_income_min` is the ATO's suggested level for letting income). Keep booking calendars, price history, enquiry responses, platform statements, evidence of the market rate for any discounted stay, and periods of private use, per property, for `bookkeeping.record_retention_years`.

## SERR judgement rules

- **What it is.** Operators of electronic distribution platforms report short-term accommodation payments to the ATO twice a year (TAA 1953 Sch 1 Subdiv 396-B, s 396-55 table item 15, from `shortstay.serr_start_short_term_accommodation`). The instrument is LI 2025/5. It replaced the 2024 determination. It has no 90-day limit: never say SERR only covers stays of a set number of days. The 2023 instrument that used a consecutive-days definition of relevant accommodation is superseded.
- **What platforms report.** Platform identity; seller name, date of birth, contact details and payment account; activity code and property type; gross income including GST, fees and commissions, refunds for cancellations through the platform, the GST amount and number of bookings; fees and commissions withheld; and per property the address and nights booked and attended.
- **What it means for a host.** The ATO holds gross receipts, fees and nights per property, so a host who declares the net payout is visibly under-reporting. SERR is a reporting regime only: it withholds no tax, registers no one for GST, and gives the host no filing duty. The platform's report can be for the manager, not the owner, where a manager holds the listing; the owner still declares gross rent from the manager's statements. Earlier ATO accommodation data-matching obtained block-out dates, prices and minimum-night rules, so the available-on-commercial-terms evidence can be tested as well as income.
- **Not reported** (LI 2025/5): a supplier that is a listed entity or government body; a substantial supplier (`shortstay.serr_substantial_supplier_value`, GST inclusive over 12 months, prorated for a new supplier); a substantial property (`shortstay.serr_substantial_property_transactions`, prorated for a new listing); a two-platform chain where the other reports and the ATO was notified; a mere booking with no price and no platform payment; and a supplier with no Australian link on all four tests together. No report does not mean no income.
- **Due dates** are the platform's, not the host's: `shortstay.serr_due_month_period_jul_dec` with `shortstay.serr_due_day_period_jul_dec`, and the same pair for the Jan to Jun period. `serr_report_check` moves a date that is a Saturday, Sunday or a public holiday for the whole of any State, the ACT or the NT to the first business day after (TAA 1953 Sch 1 s 388-52): `statutory_due` is the 31 January or 31 July date, `due` is the rolled date, and `business_day_roll` gives the reason (31 July 2027, a Saturday, becomes Tuesday 3 August 2027 because Monday 2 August is Picnic Day in the NT). Quote `due` and mention `statutory_due`. A date after the public holiday data comes back unrolled with an AU-GEN-004 entry in `field_refusals`: the scope result still stands, but quote that entry's message for the date and do not present it as checked. The six-monthly periods come from a 2023 instrument; the Act's default period is a financial year. Late or false reports attract penalties for the platform.
- **Bali and other overseas property.** The reporting rule needs a supply connected with Australia and the ATO reporting guide is built for Australian addresses. Whether an Australian resident's overseas bookings are reported is not settled: AU-SS-002. The income is assessable either way. For an open question (how to declare it, credit for foreign tax, can I claim depreciation or interest) or thin facts (a rounded net-of-tax figure, no gross and cost split), escalate: open with AU-RENT-005 verbatim, orientation in words only, no offset amount, no net Australian result, no confirmed or leaned deduction, no calculator, hand-off to a registered tax agent with cross-border experience. Only where the owner has stated gross rent, each cost, the foreign tax paid, owner or family use and any loan, and wants a computed result, work it from those amounts with `foreign_rental_net` under the rental-property skill (its overseas path), with AU-RENT-005 quoted for the parts it does not check. Do not call `short_stay_apportionment` or `rental_property_result` for overseas property.

## Lead-with rules: refuse first, orient second

These rules apply only to the three situations below. For every other question, answer firmly what the sources settle (for example an owner's own lease of one unit to a hotel operator is input taxed, with no credits, and outside GST turnover) and escalate only the part that is unresolved.

These three situations are answered by opening with the code and its message, quoted exactly as in the refusals data. Only then add short orientation in words. The first thing the reader sees is the code and its message, before any headline or "short answer". A number in the opening paragraph, or a confident conclusion, is a defect.

- **Host who says they are a foreign resident, may be one, or whose residency is unresolved** (moved overseas, works abroad, splits time between countries). Take the host's own statement as a claim to be confirmed, not a settled fact, unless dates, days in Australia, ties and intention have been given and the question is a plain computation. Lead with AU-RES-004 verbatim, as the first thing in the answer even if `individual-tax`, `rental-property`, `cgt` or `residency-cross-border` also fire (each of them defers to this rule); use AU-RES-001 instead where a treaty or dual residence is in play (messages in `data/refusals/residency.yaml`). Residency decides which rates, thresholds and main residence rules apply, so no tax figure of any kind is given: no tax payable, no rate application, no CGT illustration, no Medicare amount. Orientation can say in words only that residency changes the rules for rent, the tax-free threshold, Medicare and the main residence exemption on sale, and that the ATO material must be checked. Hand the residency question to `residency-cross-border` and the review to a registered tax agent with cross-border experience. Never call `residency_indicators` with assumed or invented facts to reach a view on residency; ask for the facts and hand the question over. Do not run this skill's or any other skill's calculators (including `individual_income_tax`, `capital_gain` and `main_residence_exemption`) on the rent or the sale until residency is settled, and do not work an illustrative example with made-up figures.
- **SA short-term rental register, or any duty rule specific to short-stay use** (fee, start date, licensing, whether short-stay changes stamp duty or land tax). Lead with AU-SS-003 verbatim. Do not say whether any register, fee, start date or short-stay duty rule exists, does not exist, or applies; the primary pages read do not settle it, and secondary reports are not verified. Point to RevenueSA, the SA Government and the council for the position. If the buyer also wants a general residential duty figure, it may be run through `state-taxes-sa`, but it must be labelled as a general residential figure that does not cover how any short-stay classification would be treated, which is unverified. Never lead with that figure.
- **Sale of a home that was let short-stay.** Renting the home is income-producing use, so the default is a partial main residence exemption under ITAA 1997 s 118-190, not a full exemption. Never open with, or conclude, that the gain is fully exempt. Set out the ATO apportionment method in words (gain measured from the relevant value, times the floor area used for income, times days used to produce income over the days in the period from first income use to sale). Where the home was first used to produce income after 20 August 1996 and would then have been fully exempt, the cost base resets to market value at that time (s 118-192), which needs a valuation: the owner's own estimate is not a valuation, so ask for one and treat any figure using an estimate as provisional (AU-CGT-006). The absence choice (s 118-145, treating the home as the main residence while away, subject to conditions) is a possible election that depends on the facts and on no other home being nominated; mention it as something for the agent to confirm, not as the outcome. The count of days used to produce income has two readings that the sources do not choose between, so present both and escalate the choice to a registered tax agent rather than asserting one. Reading A, days actually let: only the nights booked count as days used to produce income; the ATO steps speak of "the number of days you used your home to produce assessable income" (ATO "Using your home for rental or business", step 4). Reading B, days available for letting: a home genuinely available to let on commercial terms, for example a whole house left available while the owner is overseas, counts for every day it was available from first income use to sale; s 118-190(2) reduces the exemption by what is reasonable having regard to the interest that would have been deductible, and interest is deductible while the property is held or available to produce rent (the same idea as "days held to produce income" in PCG 2026/2 paras 13 to 15 for the income tax side). Where a figure is given, show both, mark each provisional pending the valuation and the agent's choice, and say plainly that the income tax test for available days is not something the ATO page applies to the CGT proportion in terms. Hand the arithmetic to `cgt` and do not compute the taxable part here.

## Output

End every answer with this working paper (CONVENTIONS section 8):
